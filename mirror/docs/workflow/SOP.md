# Video Editing SOP — How We Brief and Build
14 November 2026 (owner paste, saved verbatim)

This SOP teaches a new editor how this client briefs, what every request really means, and the rules each video must pass before it is sent.

## 1. How the client briefs
The client writes short, fast, informal messages, often voice-typed, with typos and no punctuation. Read for intent, not wording. A short message usually carries a big expectation: "do it properly" means every rule in this SOP, not a quick patch.

What a typical brief contains:

- The inputs. An outline doc and a script doc for the video, and later the voiceover (often in 2 parts that must be joined cleanly).
- The first deliverable. Almost always "show me the Intro + Case 1 first ASAP". Nothing else gets built until that preview is approved.
- The style references: the chosen style guide (Farrell, Nocturn, Abyssal or combined), given once, then assumed forever.
- The standard. "Top tier", "banger", "proper video", "pay attention to every single detail". They review frame by frame and send screenshots with timestamps.

How they give feedback:

- Screenshots plus a timestamp ("at 1:14 don't use animations like that"). The note on one moment applies to every similar moment in the video.
- Rules arrive once and stay. If they say "no glitch" or "no captions" once, it is banned in every future video too. Keep a running rules list.
- They change direction between videos. Each new video must look slightly different from the last (see section 4).
- Mid-task messages. They send new notes while you work ("change that title, make it smooth"). Fold them into the current round; don't wait.

How they want to be kept informed:

- Short updates every few minutes while working, in plain words.
- Honest ETAs. They ask "how much time left" often. Measure progress (chunks done vs total) and give a real clock time. Never guess optimistically.
- Speed matters. They say "fast, use the full power of the Mac". Run long renders in the background and work on the next thing meanwhile.

## 2. The workflow, step by step
Every video runs the same 8 steps; steps 5 and 6 repeat until the client approves.

1. Analyse the outline and script as soon as they arrive, before the voiceover. List every case, what footage is allowed for each, what is image-only, which foreign text needs translating, and every "handle with care" note.
2. Gather media early. Download allowed clips, capture article pages, collect licence-safe photos, and log each item with its source, licence and best timestamps.
3. Build the video's own look (palette, transitions, title treatment, a motif) so it differs from the last video.
4. Voiceover arrives. Join the parts cleanly, transcribe with word timings, split it into case ranges, and find the real pauses.
5. Intro + Case 1 preview. Build it beat by beat to the voiceover, check it, and send it with a short note of what is in it.
6. Feedback round. Read every note and screenshot, turn each into a rule (section 7), fix all similar moments, not only the one shown, and resend.
7. The remaining cases, built in the approved style, checked case by case.
8. Full join: intro first, all cases, outro; final checks on duration, black frames, loudness; send.

The Intro + Case 1 preview is the most important step: it sets the style for the whole video, so it gets the most care and the most checking.

## 3. Footage and licence rules
The client fears copyright strikes, so there are no exceptions and no "client's call" items. If a case has no clean video, build it from pages and motion graphics, or replace the case.

| Allowed | Banned |
|---|---|
| The subject's own uploads (when their channel passes the check) | TV, radio, news and web-TV uploads |
| Small, inactive reupload channels with no logos or licensing notes | Police, sheriff, state and foreign-government video releases |
| Wikimedia Commons photos (log author and licence) | Clips sold by agencies (ViralHog, Jukin, Storyful, Newsflare and similar) |
| Public-domain archive.org items | Professional filmers' footage; photos that need permission |
| US federal works (NASA, USGS, NTSB, NOAA, NPS, Coast Guard) | Anything with a visible watermark |
| Screenshots of article and web pages (shown as pages) | Footage behind a licensing note, or from a channel that claims copyright |

Privacy, always:

- Never show children's faces. Blur or skip the shot.
- Private people (followers, victims, bystanders) get no names and no faces: blur names on pages, crop or skip shots where they appear.
- Living subjects: never diagnose or label them; stick to sourced facts.
- Follow every "handle with care" note in the outline (for example: skip a sexual segment, don't repeat violent instructions beyond a short quoted phrase).
- Don't bypass captchas or bot checks to capture a page; use another source.

Keep a media log per case: file, what it shows, which script line it fits, best timestamps, licence and credit, and risks (faces, logos).

## 5. Editing rules
These rules come from the client's past feedback. Each one was a correction, so breaking any of them costs a revision round.

**Pacing**
- The voiceover runs at its natural speed. Don't stretch pauses; add silence only where the subject's own voice plays or at 2–3 big story turns.
- Never let the video feel sleepy, even in a "fall asleep to" video. Never hold one animation long.
- Intro: something changes every 1.5–4 s (a cut, a new element, a highlight). Body: shots of about 3–7 s, and anything held longer keeps moving.
- But no flicker-fast visuals: no shot under about 2.5 s, and any on-screen text the viewer must read stays at least 3.5 s.

**Text**
- Text is rare: places, dates, numbers, names, keywords. Never captions that repeat the voiceover.
- No boxes, no underlines, no word lists, nothing in the corners, no credits on screen.
- Entrances: a blur-dissolve, a fade with the picture, or a cut. Text doesn't bounce, slide or type itself in (the one exception is a Brew-style typewriter time stamp).

**Articles and highlights**
- Pages are full-screen and flat (no 3D tilt), with shallow depth of field and constant drift. The highlighted words must always be fully inside the frame.
- Highlights are red with a soft glow (blue where red doesn't suit). The highlight sweeps across in sync with the narrator saying those words.
- Markers are measured onto the actual words (word positions from the page), never guessed, and never spill outside the words.
- Blur private names and ads; never show pages with captcha screens or popups.

**Translations**
- Any foreign text the viewer must read gets the translation card, held on screen at least 3.5 s. Highlight the original first, then show the card.

**Images and footage**
- Images stay fully inside the frame; nothing is cut off awkwardly.
- Every still drifts slowly (push or slow rotation); nothing sits locked.
- Use real footage and photos that match the exact line being spoken. Use pages only where needed, not as filler.
- Maps only where geography matters, and done well (a real satellite image with a drawn route), not on every case.

**Banned devices**
- Glitch, flash frames, VHS/CRT looks, stamps, rolling clocks or timers.
- Rounded dark "cards" with lists of words, names with accent underlines, a plain centred title with a thin line: these read as "AI templates".
- Using the same device again and again in one video.
- Watermarks and children's faces.

**Title**
- The title must be smooth: lines rising out of their baselines with a soft blur, a slow push-in, and a soft exit. No hard pops.

## 6. Audio rules
- Voiceover is never processed. No EQ, compressor or limiter: it arrives mastered, and processing caused an audible crackle behind the voice. Only match its level.
- Joining a two-part voiceover: add about 0.45 s of silence between the parts and check the join by ear.
- The subject's own voice plays in a gap in the narration, lip-synced to their clip, slightly under the narrator's level.
- Music sits about 15–25 dB under the voice (never closer than the master SOP 7.7 gate) and never dies, ducks gently while the narrator speaks, and changes track at story turns. No swells right before words.
- SFX are sparse and real: a low hit on keyword or number cards, a soft whoosh on date cards, typewriter clicks on time stamps. No highlight sounds, no glitch sounds.
- Final loudness: −16 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP (master SOP 7.7).

## 7. Feedback decoder
What the client writes, and what it actually asks for. Apply each fix to every similar moment in the video, not just the timestamp shown.

| Client says | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Looks like AI templates" | Generic cards, lists, underlined names, tidy small text | Rebuild from the measured reference devices (section 4); every frame shows one specific thing for that exact line |
| "Pacing is way too slow" / "feels sleepy" | Pauses stretched, shots held too long | Remove added silences; change the visual every 2–5 s; keep holds moving |
| "No quick visuals" (earlier video) | Flicker-fast cuts, text gone before it can be read | Shots at least 2.5–3 s, readable text at least 3.5 s |
| "Voice sounds different / glitch in the background" | Voice processing artifacts | Use the untouched voiceover, level only |
| "Proper Farrell style" | Angled full-screen articles, huge Arial Narrow text, B&W with one accent | See section 5, Articles and Text |
| "Make the title smooth" | Hard pops or snappy motion on the title | Slow rise with blur, push-in, soft exit |
| "Don't use stuff from previous videos" | The last video's look is being reused | New palette, transitions, title and motif per video |
| "Don't overuse maps" | Maps used as filler | Only where geography matters, done as a real map with a route |
| "You missed info from the outline" | Outline facts not shown on screen | Show the outline's facts visually (pages, numbers, dates, footage) on the matching lines |
| "Add relevant footage and images" | Too many graphics, too little real material | Find more allowed clips, photos and pages that match each line (section 3) |
| "Do it properly / top tier" | Every rule in this SOP, checked | Run the full checklist (section 8) before sending |

## 8. Checklist before sending, and reporting
Tick every box before a render goes to the client.

- [ ] Duration matches the plan; voiceover and picture are in sync to the end
- [ ] No black gaps, flashes or sudden jumps (scan the whole file, not just a watch-through)
- [ ] Loudness −16 LUFS; voice clean (no crackle); the subject's own voice clips lip-synced
- [ ] Every highlight lands on the spoken words and stays inside the frame
- [ ] Every translation card stays on screen at least 3.5 s
- [ ] No children's faces, private names, followers' faces or watermarks anywhere (pages and thumbnails too)
- [ ] No banned devices (section 5) and no device repeated too often
- [ ] Each case's outline facts and "handle with care" notes are respected
- [ ] The look differs from the previous video
- [ ] One or two small contact sheets checked by eye

When you send it, include:

- The file and its length
- A short list of what is in it (the key moments, any of the subject's own clips)
- What you could not use and why (for example "no licence-safe raid footage, so it's built from pages and graphics")
- What changed since the last version, if it's a revision

While working: a short update every few minutes, and an ETA based on measured progress, given as a clock time.
