## CASE 01 · The tank that folded · Switz City, Indiana

[SW] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fmrxFcTTv-c>

White Farms, Switz City, Indiana. July 2017. At about one in the afternoon on Monday the 31st, an overhead tank full of corn started emptying out of its side. Before long, the people nearby could see the steel underneath it buckling.

▶ RAW · [SW] 0:30–0:58 · [visual: the overhead tank on its steel superstructure, corn pouring out of its side] · ~28s · mute the expletive near 0:32

A tank like this sits up on legs so a truck can drive underneath and load from above. All the weight of the grain sits on that steel structure.

▶ RAW · [SW] 1:20–1:48 · [visual: corn still pouring, the superstructure under the tank starting to strain] · ~28s · the ASR has the filmer saying "it's buckling the frame of the superstructure" in this stretch

Grain dust is the part farm safety people worry about most. It's the fine powder that comes off corn when it moves. When enough of it hangs in the air, it can flash in an instant.

▶ RAW · [SW] 1:48–2:06 · [visual: the tank leans and drops, the filmer backs away] · ~18s

According to the farm, the tank struck a power line on the way down, and that's what lit the dust. No agency report backs that up, so it stays their explanation.

▶ RAW · [SW] 2:06–2:25 · [visual: the flash in the dust cloud, then the cloud settling over the yard] · ~19s

Nobody was hurt. Farm safety trainers now use this collapse as a lesson. They spend more time on the flash at the end than on the fall. The flash is the part that has cost lives in grain elevators again and again.

## CASE 02 · The jib came down · Sydney, Australia

The UTS tower crane, Sydney, November 2012. It stood over a building site on Broadway, at the University of Technology, and around mid morning its cab caught fire. Hundreds of people stopped in the street and at office windows. According to CNN, firefighters believed diesel was involved.

▶ RAW · [UTS] 0:06–0:34 · [visual: the tower crane above the skyline, black smoke pouring from its cab, the jib still up] · ~28s · the fire shows as smoke from the cab for most of this clip

In the cab was the operator, Glen May, who was sixty. He fought the fire first. Then, before he got down, he went back into the burning cab and swung the jib round. Now it hung over the building site instead of over Broadway and the crowds.

▶ RAW · [KO] 0:40–1:08 · [visual: the smoking crane from an office window west of the site, beside a tall building] · ~28s · onlookers' remarks only, no narration

For well over twenty minutes, the crane stood over the city with its cab burning and the jib still up. Nobody below could do anything about it.

▶ RAW · [FULL] 10:00–10:28 · [visual: the burning crane from the office window, smoke pouring from the cab] · ~28s · ⚠ SCRUB, any stretch inside 0:50–26:12 works

A dogman on the site, Nuno Nunes, climbed the crane to reach him and brought him down. The jib was held at its angle by a luffing cable, and that cable ran straight through the heat.

▶ RAW · [JA] 0:01–0:22 · [visual: the burning crane on a long zoom from the CBD, the jib dropping at about 0:17] · ~21s · silent clip, the uploader removed the sound

The cable burned through, and the jib folded onto the unfinished building, exactly where May had put it, and not across Broadway.

▶ RAW · [KO] 2:16–2:40 · [visual: the jib coming down onto the unfinished building at about 2:30] · ~24s · ⚠ SCRUB, collapse time from the uploader's description

Once the jib was down, the flames finally broke out of the cab.

Lend Lease, which ran the site, said nobody was injured, and May and Nunes were both later given bravery awards. No finding on what started the fire was ever published. A safe ending that depends on one man climbing back into a burning cab is luck with a name on it.

## CASE 03 · Nine days at Alemoa · Santos, Brazil

[EP] <https://youtu.be/8NU0lyaBcNQ>
[SAN] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CUyyoBd6fbc>
[DR] <https://youtu.be/p8VDxN-q4-U>

Alemoa, Brazil, 2015. Ultracargo's fuel terminal sits right next to the port of Santos, the biggest port in the country. The fire started at about ten in the morning on the 2nd of April, and it didn't stay in one tank. By the 4th, a sixth tank had caught.

▶ RAW · [EP] 0:00–0:29 · [visual: phone view over vegetation and power lines, tanks burning with orange flame under black smoke] · ~29s

By then, according to Maritime Executive, four tanks were burning. You can't put out a tank of fuel with water, because the fuel floats on top and keeps burning. Crews have to smother the surface in foam, in huge volumes, and keep cooling the tanks next door so they don't go as well.

▶ RAW · [SAN] 0:04–0:32 · [visual: a tank burning at its rim, a long arc of water from a cannon truck crossing the frame, black smoke behind] · ~28s · filming day not stated, don't call it the first hour

Water cannon trucks were brought in to keep the jets on the tanks around the clock.

▶ RAW · [SAN] 1:30–1:58 · [visual: flames at the tank rim under the water and foam streams] · ~28s

The water and foam had to go somewhere, and a lot of it ran into the estuary and the mangroves around the terminal.

▶ RAW · [DR] 0:04–0:32 · [visual: drone over the tank farm, several tanks burning with open flame under smoke, water streams] · ~28s

Ultracargo said there were no victims, and the area around the terminal was evacuated.

▶ RAW · [DR] 1:31–1:50 · [visual: tanks in flames from above] · ~19s

The tanks burned for nine days. No cause was given at the time, and what came instead was the bill. CETESB, the São Paulo state environment agency, fined Ultracargo more than twenty two million reais. It cited the deaths of thousands of fish in the estuary and the Casqueiro river. Federal prosecutors also charged the company over the pollution. A terminal like this sits beside the port because ships unload straight into its tanks. So when it burns, it burns next to everything the port depends on.

## CASE 04 · The blending plant · Waxahachie, Texas

[MAG] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UW25SNPKX5I>

Magnablend. Waxahachie, Texas. October 2011. The fire broke out shortly before eleven in the morning, and the black smoke darkened the sky for miles.

▶ RAW · [MF] 0:00–0:27 · [visual: from a parking lot, flames at the base of the black column over the plant, parked trucks in front] · ~27s

Magnablend makes custom chemicals for oil fields, farming, water treatment and other industries. It blends and packages them on site, so a lot of different chemicals pass through one building. Between twenty five and thirty people were inside that morning, mixing a wastewater treatment product. According to the company, the mix gave off too much steam, and a spark nearby lit it. That's the company's own account of it.

## CASE 05 · Oil cars in the river · Lynchburg, Virginia

[OF] <https://youtu.be/Z1SH1pn4jeY>
[LYN] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q_wWk68kgxQ>
[BR] <https://youtu.be/FcsvPpDR7d0>

Downtown Lynchburg, Virginia. 2014. Early in the afternoon of the 30th of April, a CSX train carrying crude oil was running through the middle of the city, along the James River. Part of it came off the rails beside the water.

▶ RAW · [OF] 0:00–0:20 · [visual: high office-window view over downtown rooftops, the tank cars burning with tall flames under a black column] · ~20s · stop before the window frame edge at about 0:20

About twelve to fourteen tank cars had left the rails. In 2014, crude was moving across North America by rail in numbers it never had before. The summer before, a runaway oil train had destroyed the centre of Lac-Mégantic in Quebec, and forty seven people were lost. Everybody in that city knew what it could have been.

▶ RAW · [LYN] 0:01–0:29 · [visual: from the riverbank, the black column rising over the water, flames at its base beside the tracks] · ~28s · filmer not identified

At least three cars split open, and some of the oil ran into the river. Then the fire department made a call that sounds strange the first time you hear it. They let it burn.

▶ RAW · [LYN] 0:29–0:59 · [visual: the column and the flames at its base by the river] · ~30s

Next to a river, it was the right call. Pouring water and foam onto burning crude at the water's edge pushes the oil straight into the water supply. The area around the tracks was evacuated while it burned.

▶ RAW · [BR] 0:39–1:07 · [visual: from a car window on the bridge, the burning tank cars by the river] · ~28s

Nobody was injured, and the city said its drinking water wasn't affected. Virginia's environment department said nearly thirty thousand gallons of crude escaped, and most of it burned off. Hold on to those two words.

## CASE 06 · Two in the morning · Columbus, Ohio

[COL] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rA84gZA-myg>
[CC] <https://youtu.be/aMNDM0qLVSs>
[SE] <https://youtu.be/XqMxM92efxE>

Two in the morning, Columbus, Ohio. July 2012. Around Fifth Avenue and Fourth Street, on the edge of Weinland Park and Italian Village, people woke to an orange glow behind the streetlights. A Norfolk Southern freight train of ninety eight cars had been heading north of downtown, on its way to Chicago. Seventeen of its cars had come off the track.

▶ RAW · [COL] 0:02–0:28 · [visual: a dark street, a streetlight, the orange glow of burning rail cars beyond it] · ~26s · mute the filmers' language

One car was punctured and spilled ethanol, and the ethanol fed the fire. Then the explosions started.

▶ RAW · [COL] 0:28–0:56 · [visual: the fire flaring up behind the streetlight at about 0:29] · ~28s · don't label the flare-up as the main blast

Two more ethanol cars caught fire and blew apart, and everyone within about a mile was told to leave. About a hundred residents were evacuated in the middle of the night.

According to the Columbus Dispatch, there was styrene on that train as well, and it never caught. That part rarely gets mentioned.

▶ RAW · [SE] 0:00–0:30 · [visual: close night view of the burning rail cars, large flames through trees] · ~30s

The NTSB counted two people injured. Its probable cause, issued in 2014, was a broken rail, with the damage put at one point two million dollars. Ethanol has a reputation as the clean fuel, and a tank car of it is still an enormous amount of fuel. Two of them going up was enough to empty a neighbourhood. That's broken rail number two.

## CASE 07 · Fireballs in the snow · Mount Carbon, West Virginia

[RW] <https://youtu.be/7IAB1rIqELY>
[MC] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XwACBh1IWWw>
[SH] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OXS4MvtMtX4>
[SH3] <https://youtu.be/A7vUJdcCpxU>

Mount Carbon, West Virginia. February 2015. It was about twenty past one in the afternoon, in a snowstorm. A CSX train of a hundred and nine tank cars was carrying about three million gallons of Bakken crude along the Kanawha River, and it came off the tracks. It was doing thirty three miles an hour, under both the legal limit and the railroad's own cold weather limit.

▶ RAW · [RW] 0:01–0:25 · [visual: fire beyond snowy rooftops, then a fireball at about 0:15] · ~24s · mute the family's shouting, profanity

Twenty seven cars derailed. About twenty leaked, and at least fourteen caught fire or exploded. The first fireball came twenty five minutes after the derailment, and the last of thirteen came more than ten hours later.

▶ RAW · [MC] 0:13–0:41 · [visual: flames on the far bank through bare branches, then a fireball filling the frame] · ~28s · a tank-car explosion, not the derailment moment

A house near the tracks burned down, and at least one car went into the river. The whole town of Boomer was evacuated, and two water treatment plants shut their intakes. Hundreds of families had to leave, and the fire burned for four days.

▶ RAW · [SH] 1:05–1:33 · [visual: the snowy riverbank, the burning cars across the water] · ~28s

The Federal Railroad Administration found a broken rail with a vertical split in the rail head. That defect had shown up in inspection data in December 2014, and again in January 2015. Nobody went out and checked it by hand.

▶ RAW · [SH3] 0:08–0:36 · [visual: across the snowy field and river, the tank cars burning with flame under black smoke] · ~28s

These weren't old tank cars either. They were built to a newer industry standard from 2011, called CPC 1232, which was supposed to be the safer design. There's a second question with these trains, which is what's inside the cars. In 2014, US regulators said Bakken crude from North Dakota tended to be more volatile than other crude oils. The oil industry pushed back on parts of that claim.

▶ RAW · [SH3] 2:00–2:28 · [visual: the tank cars still burning across the river, flames under black smoke] · ~28s

CSX said one person was treated for possible breathing problems. CSX and its inspection contractor, Sperry, were each fined twenty five thousand dollars. Two months of data pointed at the exact piece of steel that failed. Broken rail number three.

## CASE 08 · The gauge that lied · Bayamón, Puerto Rico

[CCT] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dmHIqsUgyUA>
[KR] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zP8-j4EhpnA>
[WE] <https://youtu.be/IhGPLhO3G-c>

Caribbean Petroleum, Bayamón, Puerto Rico. October 2009. Twenty past midnight, on the 23rd.

▶ RAW · [CCT] 0:05–0:33 · [visual: security-camera playback, the blast flash and shockwave hitting the terminal's cameras] · ~28s · posted by a CCTV contractor, supporting angle only

A ship was offloading gasoline into a storage tank, and the tank's gauge said there was room. According to the Chemical Safety Board, the gauge was faulty and the tank was already overflowing. The gasoline formed a vapour cloud, the cloud reached electrical equipment, and the blast registered like a magnitude two point eight earthquake. It was heard eleven miles away.

▶ RAW · [KR] 0:08–0:36 · [visual: a wall of fire behind the terminal fence at night, burning tanks lighting the sky] · ~28s · mute the bystanders' audio

This is the first CSB case on the iceberg. The Chemical Safety Board is a small independent federal agency that looks into chemical accidents the way the NTSB looks into crashes. It can't fine anyone. It can only find out what happened and tell everyone else.

▶ RAW · [KR] 1:30–1:58 · [visual: burning tanks and flame walls, fire engines arriving] · ~28s · ⚠ SCRUB, beat estimated within the watched range

The usual fix is a second high level alarm that works separately from the gauge. That way, one broken instrument can't hide an overflow on its own.

▶ RAW · [KR] 2:30–2:58 · [visual: the flame walls towering over the fire engines at the terminal] · ~28s · ⚠ SCRUB, mute the bystanders

Eleven tanks were destroyed, and the fire burned until the 25th. The pressure wave damaged buildings around the terminal and hit cars moving on the roads nearby. By daylight, the tanks along the perimeter were still burning.

▶ RAW · [WE] 0:03–0:31 · [visual: daylight through the perimeter fence, burning tanks and flame walls under black smoke] · ~28s

Three people were injured, nobody lost their life, and about fifteen hundred residents were evacuated. CAPECO filed for bankruptcy the following August, and the US Environmental Protection Agency took over the cleanup. The CSB's final report came out in October 2015, six years later, and called for automatic overfill prevention on big fuel tanks. One gauge reading wrong at midnight took out the whole terminal.

## CASE 09 · Thirty eight cars · East Palestine, Ohio

[FS] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=95igiL5rmhI>
[MR] <https://youtu.be/4U0zFodfvro>
[MR2] <https://youtu.be/cgTJfAax_vg>
[ET] <https://youtu.be/BxfQy0jo9EU>

East Palestine, Ohio. February 2023. Almost everyone has heard of this one, mostly for what happened three days later. It happened just before nine on a Friday night, on the edge of the village.

▶ RAW · [FS] 0:13–0:41 · [visual: drone over the tracks at night, the derailed cars piled together and burning under black smoke] · ~28s · cut the 11-second intro and logo slate

A Norfolk Southern freight was passing through near the Pennsylvania line when a bearing on one of its hopper cars overheated and failed. Detectors beside the track are there to catch a hot bearing. The crew got no warning until the train passed one, and by then it was too late to stop. Thirty eight cars came off.

▶ RAW · [MR] 0:13–0:41 · [visual: night aerial, the line of wrecked cars burning in a long wall of flame] · ~28s

Eleven were tank cars carrying hazardous materials, and three were breached. Those three were an older tank car design, the DOT one eleven, which safety investigators had criticised for years for splitting open in derailments. The village ordered everyone within a mile to leave.

▶ RAW · [MR2] 2:49–3:17 · [visual: close night passes over the burning cars with emergency lights] · ~28s

On the 6th of February, five tank cars of vinyl chloride were deliberately vented and burned. This car was still burning the day after the derailment.

▶ RAW · [ET] 0:02–0:30 · [visual: close drone hover over a derailed tank car still burning in the wreck pile] · ~28s · footage from 4 Feb

The NTSB reported no deaths and no injuries. In June 2024, the NTSB found the vent and burn wasn't necessary to prevent a tank car failure. It said the decision rested on incomplete and misleading information from Norfolk Southern officials and contractors. In September 2024, a judge gave final approval to a six hundred million dollar settlement for residents and businesses within twenty miles. The derailment was the accident. The fire everyone remembers was a choice.

## CASE 10 · The white cloud · Woods Cross, Utah

[SE] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yDmuaR6rkgU>

Silver Eagle refinery, Woods Cross, Utah. November 2009. Woods Cross is a small city just north of Salt Lake, and the refinery sits right next to a neighbourhood. On the 4th of November, a pipe on the unit called a hydrotreater ruptured.

▶ RAW · [SE] 0:03–0:28 · [visual: refinery surveillance camera, the unit quiet, then the hydrogen escaping] · ~25s · official CSB release, ⚠ SCRUB on where the vapour first shows, never use the CSB animations

That cloud is hydrogen. A hydrotreater strips sulfur out of fuel by running it through hydrogen at high pressure and high temperature. So when a pipe on one fails, it doesn't leak slowly. It lets go, and hydrogen needs almost nothing to ignite.

▶ RAW · [SE] 0:28–0:52 · [visual: the fireball tearing across the unit at about 0:29] · ~24s

Four workers were knocked off their feet, and none of them was seriously hurt. The blast wave carried on into the neighbourhood and damaged more than a hundred homes, and one was pushed off its foundation.

▶ RAW · [SE] 0:52–1:20 · [visual: the unit burning with black smoke] · ~28s

In 2014 the CSB found the pipe wall had corroded down to thirty nine thousandths of an inch. There was no record of it ever being checked for corrosion.

▶ RAW · [SE] 1:20–1:43 · [visual: the fire continuing in the unit] · ~23s

It wasn't even the refinery's first serious fire that year. In January, a vapour cloud from a naphtha tank had flashed into a fire, and four workers were badly hurt. Corrosion is slow, which means there were years in which a single inspection would have found it. Three years later, the same failure turned up in a much bigger refinery in California.

## CASE 11 · Nineteen workers in the cloud · Richmond, California

[CHV] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hewMRcrOI0s>
[MO] <https://youtu.be/syyBHIhGpYU>
[CU] <https://youtu.be/COcdbVWddic>

Chevron, Richmond, California. August 2012. In Crude Unit Number Four, a pipe had started to leak, and crews had been sent out to deal with it.

▶ RAW · [CHV] 0:09–0:37 · [visual: the unit normal, then white vapour starting to spread] · ~28s · official CSB release, exclude the CSB animation

While they were working on it, the pipe ruptured. According to the CSB, nineteen workers were in danger, and several of them were inside that cloud.

▶ RAW · [CHV] 0:37–1:05 · [visual: the white vapour cloud filling the unit] · ~28s

All nineteen got out.

▶ RAW · [CHV] 3:16–3:44 · [visual: fire and black smoke rising from the unit] · ~28s · ⚠ SCRUB on the exact ignition point

The corrosion here has a name, sulfidation. Sulfur compounds in crude oil eat at carbon steel pipe from the inside, slowly and evenly. The wall thins along a whole length, instead of in one spot that's easy to find. A pipe can look fine from the outside right up to the day it fails. By evening, the smoke was rising over the 580 freeway.

▶ RAW · [MO] 0:55–1:18 · [visual: from a car on the 580, the refinery tower in flames under a black column] · ~23s

The smoke drifted over the city, and more than fifteen thousand residents sought medical care. Those people weren't on the refinery payroll. They were its neighbours.

▶ RAW · [CU] 0:20–0:47 · [visual: the smoke column rising over a residential street, flames at its base] · ~27s · cut 0:49–0:59, TV alert screen

The CSB found Chevron hadn't identified sulfidation as a likely way for that pipe to fail. Chevron also had no formal guidance for responding to a leak, so the crews walked up to it. It also reported that Chevron's own specialists had recommended upgrading piping like this years before. In 2013, Chevron pleaded no contest to six misdemeanour charges and agreed to pay two million dollars. Cal OSHA, California's workplace safety agency, proposed a fine of nearly a million, and Chevron appealed it. Two refineries, three years apart, and the same quiet problem in the steel.

## CASE 13 · The man inside · Crockett, California

[NU] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-u4BeyqyP8o>
[JAG] <https://youtu.be/PtcLYfDRFKs>

October 2019, Crockett, California. NuStar's fuel terminal sits in the hills by the water near Rodeo, below the road through the hills. A lot of reports called it a refinery. It's a storage terminal, and that matters for who was on site that day.

▶ RAW · [NU] 0:02–0:30 · [visual: the tank farm from the hills, one tank pouring black smoke with flame at its base] · ~28s · the fire after the first blast

A major explosion had lit two tanks of denatured ethanol. The terminal was evacuated, and broken lines were leaking diesel and jet fuel.

▶ RAW · [JAG] 0:18–0:46 · [visual: roadside view from the hills road, black smoke over the terminal, trucks passing] · ~28s

About twenty minutes after everyone was supposed to be out, firefighters found something nobody had planned for. A man was still inside. He was a contractor, there to do scheduled maintenance, and they pulled him out. He wasn't seriously hurt.

▶ RAW · [NU] 0:48–1:16 · [visual: close view of the burning tanks and the ground fire] · ~28s

The fireball coming up is a later flare up, not the first explosion.
