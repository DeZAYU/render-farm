## INTRO

A ride is built to scare you without ever putting you in danger. That's the whole deal. You hand over your ticket, somebody checks your harness, and you trust that every bolt and cable on the thing was looked at by someone who cared. These are twenty-five times that deal broke while somebody was filming. Some of them are slow. Riders hang face-down for two hours in Osaka. Sixty-two people sit stuck on a dark wheel over Orlando on New Year's Eve. Some are over in a second. A few still don't have an official cause. I've put the worst first. It's Friday evening at the Seville fair. Two kids are strapped into a slingshot called Steel Max, and one of its two cables is about to let go.

## CASE 03 · The Zipper cages stack up · Wilmington, Delaware

A Zipper is a long boom that turns end over end. Cages hang along it, and they flip on their own as it turns. It folds onto a trailer, which is why it turns up at county fairs and church carnivals all over America. Here, one cage had come down on top of the cage below. ▶ RAW · [ZM] 19:00–19:30 · [visual: the stacked cages at height, riders visible inside] · ~30s

▶ RAW · [ZM] 22:00–22:30 · [visual: close shots of the jammed cages with riders inside] · ~30s

That was four days before it jammed. The paperwork also showed an inspection in April 2022 after what was described as a total rebuild. High-angle rescue is the fire service's term for any job too high or too awkward for a ladder on its own. It means ropes and anchors and a lot of patience. A Zipper adds one more problem. Its cages are designed to spin, so each one had to be held still before anybody opened a door.

Staff tried pushing the train back to the platform, and it wouldn't budge. Cheetah Hunt is a launched coaster at Busch Gardens in Tampa. On the afternoon of Wednesday the 12th of February 2014, it stalled on a crest not long out of the station. The train was left sitting on that crest, high over the park.

▶ RAW · [CH] 0:12–0:31 · [visual: a ladder bucket raised beside the stalled train on the green track, riders still in their seats] · ~19s · Tampa Fire Rescue's own release, trim the logo cards at the start and end

According to CNN, the riders sat up there in the rain, in ponchos, for more than three hours.

▶ RAW · [CH] 0:46–1:16 · [visual: aerial ladders raised at the ride] · ~30s · small corner credit from 0:48, credit Tampa Fire Rescue

Tampa Fire Rescue brought two ladder companies and used a cherry picker that was already on site.

▶ RAW · [CH] 1:16–1:37 · [visual: wide shots of the train stopped high on the green track with riders aboard, two aerial ladders working beside it] · ~21s

Once crews were in place, the fire service says it took about an hour to get everyone off. The park said fifteen people were aboard. The fire service said it took sixteen off. Somebody, somewhere, miscounted. Nobody was hurt. Busch Gardens said only that the ride had malfunctioned near the loading station. It stayed shut the next day while the park worked out why.

## CASE 24 · Demon stops twice in one day · Gurnee, Illinois

[DM] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=boUFOGTCdIw>
[DJ] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1ir1XA_PmSs>

Labor Day weekend, Saturday the 1st of September 2018, at Six Flags Great America in Gurnee, Illinois. A Six Flags spokeswoman said safety sensors had stopped the Demon on its climb and the riders were walked down. That stop made the local news. According to a fan who follows the park, there was a second one that day, on the same hill, and that one didn't.

▶ RAW · [DM] 0:11–0:41 · [visual: the train halted on the lift hill under a grey sky, riders seated] · ~30s · uploader posted the same night, didn't say they filmed it

So after the first stop, the ride was back running with people on it, and it stopped again in the same place.

▶ RAW · [DJ] 0:09–0:39 · [visual: tight zoom on riders in ponchos and caps climbing out and walking down the lift-hill stairs with staff in hi-vis] · ~30s · JiggidyNDG's own footage

Walking down the lift hill is how most coaster evacuations end. The stairs run right beside the track, and staff come up and bring the riders down with them.

▶ RAW · [DJ] 1:30–2:00 · [visual: riders and staff filing down the stairs beside the stopped cars] · ~30s

A coaster is split into sections, and the control system only lets one train into each section at a time. When a sensor doesn't like something, it stops the train where it is, and on the Demon that was the lift hill. It's the system working. It still leaves people sitting in the air.

▶ RAW · [DJ] 3:43–4:13 · [visual: wide shots of the lift hill with the train halted and people on the stairs] · ~30s

Some riders were still sitting in the cars, waiting their turn.

▶ RAW · [DM] 3:05–3:35 · [visual: riders seated in the cars and people on the stairs beside the lift] · ~30s

Six Flags said everyone got off safely, and the ride closed for what it called a thorough inspection before reopening. Anyone in the queue for that second ride had no idea it had already stopped up there once that day, because nobody tells the queue. Six Flags never said how many riders were on either train or how long they waited.

## CASE 25 · Two hundred on the Sky Glider · West Allis, Wisconsin

[SK] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ijwhu8zBqpk>
[ST] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_cAzqSObyuY>
[SB] <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXZ0ziG3TnQ>

Opening night at the Wisconsin State Fair, Thursday the 5th of August 2010. The Sky Glider is a chairlift. Its open chairs run on one cable high over the midway, so you can see the whole fair from above. That evening its motor failed and the operator couldn't restart it, and some riders said it was about ten minutes before they even noticed. Firefighters came with ladders and a cherry picker.

▶ RAW · [SK] 0:22–0:52 · [visual: night shots across the midway of the stopped chairs, a fire-department bucket truck raised to them] · ~30s · posted the same night, treat as the 2010 stall

Every chair on a chairlift runs off one cable and one drive. When the motor goes, every chair stops wherever it happens to be, and some of these were hanging right over the crowd.

▶ RAW · [SK] 0:52–1:20 · [visual: the stopped chairs strung over the lit midway, the bucket raised beside them] · ~28s

There were two hundred people on it, and that's what makes a chairlift different from everything else here. A coaster keeps its riders together in one train. A chairlift spreads them out along the whole cable, a chair at a time, and every chair is its own rescue.

▶ RAW · [SK] 1:20–1:50 · [visual: the bucket at the level of the hanging chairs, more chairs strung along the cable above] · ~30s

Because it was opening night, the fair underneath was packed, so every chair that came down had an audience.

▶ RAW · [ST] 0:10–0:40 · [visual: the bucket with people in it beside the elevated station] · ~30s

Some of the chairs were stuck right beside the Ferris wheel.

▶ RAW · [SB] 0:00–0:17 · [visual: the raised bucket among the coloured hanging chairs next to the lit wheel] · ~17s

The bucket kept working its way down the line.

▶ RAW · [SK] 2:10–2:40 · [visual: the bucket truck at the stopped chairs over the midway] · ~30s

It took fire crews about two and a half hours to bring all two hundred down, and not one of them was hurt. Within two days it was reported up and running again. The same ride broke down again the next summer, but that's a separate story. I've put this one last on purpose. A motor failed, and that's all. Nobody fell and nothing burned. The crews just kept going until the last chair was empty.
