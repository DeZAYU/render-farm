On the evening of June 14, 2022, two Vermont State Police troopers drove up a dirt driveway on Scott Road in Hardwick. It's a town of about 3,000 people in the state's Northeast Kingdom. They'd come for one man. Brandon Tallman, 24, was wanted in an assault and burglary investigation from Woodbury, the next town over.

It should've been a simple pickup, and it wasn't. When the troopers took hold of Brandon, his mother, Amy, grabbed her son and wouldn't let go. Within seconds the troopers were down on the driveway wrestling with both of them.

The dashcam in a cruiser parked behind the police SUV is pointed right at the struggle. While the troopers were tied up on the ground, Brandon's father, Wayne, a 52 year old who ran an excavating business, had climbed into the family's excavator. Keep your eye on the long orange arm rising behind the cruiser, and on that bucket once it starts to swing.

Police later said Wayne moved the bucket recklessly near the troopers and their cruisers. No shot was fired, and nobody on either side was hurt.

Brandon was arrested, and so was Wayne. Amy was cited for impeding an officer. When State Police released the video nine days later, a commander admitted they don't have a scenario at the academy where they practice this one.

Wayne was first charged with attempted murder, but that count was dropped. In September 2022 he pleaded guilty to three felonies, impeding a public official and two counts of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon. The weapon was the excavator. He got two to four years, all of it suspended except 90 days in jail.

Both troopers told the court that was too light, and I can't say I blame them. Ninety days for swinging a steel bucket over two officers wrestling on the ground is a hard number to swallow. Judge Justin Jiron took the deal anyway, with one condition. Wayne can only operate an excavator under a supervisor his probation officer approves, and that supervisor can't be his son.
