Nashville's Fourth of July fireworks show had just ended in 2021 when Colin Kirby started home through Midtown in his convertible, with the top down and a friend in the passenger seat. Two more friends followed behind in a pickup truck with a dashcam running.

At about 10:15 p.m., the light turned red at 17th Avenue North and Church Street, and Kirby stopped. From the pickup it's nothing at all, just two cars waiting at a light. Pay attention to the white sedan that rolls up on his left.

Police said a passenger in the back rolled the window down and threw a lit firework into the open car. It hit Kirby in the back of the head and went off somewhere between six and ten inches from it. He burned two fingers grabbing at it.

Kirby pulled over and called 911. Apart from those fingers, he had some slight hearing loss that didn't last, and his passenger wasn't hurt. He said afterward it could've just as easily landed in his lap. The blast burned a hole through the roof and scorched the trim and a door, about $3,000 in damage.

His mother, Kathy, posted the clip the next night, and within a day it was national news. Metro Nashville Police said they wanted three people believed to be in a white Infiniti G37, for aggravated assault with a deadly weapon. They called it random, and the ATF looked into it as well, but no arrest has ever been announced.

Kirby said he'd forgiven whoever did it, but he still wanted them held accountable. I'm not sure I could forgive anybody that fast with two burned fingers.
