Most police dashcams look out at the road, but one of the two in this Tulsa cruiser points straight back at the officer driving it. It's about 7:30 on a Thursday evening, August 15, 2019, and he's heading south on Riverside Drive near East 66th Place, a wide road with a grassy median down the middle. The department's release shows both cameras side by side.

There's a phone in his hands, down near his lap. Watch his eyes on the inside camera, and count how often they drop to that screen over the next half minute.

The Jeep Grand Cherokee came from the northbound lanes, crossed the median and hit him square on the driver's side. The officer went to the hospital with a sore jaw and elbow, most likely from the airbag. The Jeep's driver wasn't hurt.

Tulsa Police released both videos about ten weeks later, and I'll give them some credit for that, since the inside camera doesn't exactly flatter their own man. People noticed the phone right away. A neighbor told local reporters it possibly could have been prevented had he not been texting.

So was he disciplined? His name was never released, and the department never said. Officially, police put the blame on the Jeep driver, and Officer Jeanne Pierce, speaking for the department, said there was nothing the officer could have done.
