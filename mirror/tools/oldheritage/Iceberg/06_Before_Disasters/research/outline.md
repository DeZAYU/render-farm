### 1. Gudauri chairlift runs backwards (2018, Gudauri, Georgia) — \~300 words

**Status / quick facts:** Friday 16 March 2018, the Sadzele chairlift (a 2007 Doppelmayr quad, top station 3,279 m), Gudauri ski resort, about 120 km north of Tbilisi. The lift stopped, then ran backwards at about twice its normal speed into the base station. Injured: reported as 8 to 12 on the day; Wikipedia and the government's later statements give 11. Among them were Swedish and Ukrainian tourists, including a pregnant Swedish woman who was airlifted to Tbilisi; her injuries were not life-threatening. No deaths. Cause per the government-ordered Bureau Veritas review (announced by Economy Minister Dimitry Kumsishvili, 23 Mar 2018): a power failure stopped the lift, then operator error let it run in reverse (he did not follow the required procedure: switch to the backup diesel drive and bring riders down). The lift had passed an inspection in December 2017. Legal: the operator was fired; the head of Gudauri Mountain Management and the deputy director of the state Mountain Resort Development Company resigned; the Interior Ministry opened a criminal investigation (no outcome found). Clip tier: AMBER — same-day repost of a tourist's phone clip; TV aired fan clips later; no agency hit; the uploader (a gaming channel) is not provably the filmer. Used by: S3 @4:14 · S8 @17:00 · S10 @20:54 · S13 @10:17 (3 niche channels: Abyssal Detective 2, Chezzoid, Stories From John). ⚠ Also claimed in the ledger by the old "25 Incidents" outline.

**Summary**

It's the middle of March 2018 at Gudauri, Georgia's best-known ski resort, high in the Caucasus. The snow is good, the sky is blue, and a crowd of skiers and snowboarders is packed around the bottom station of the Sadzele chairlift, the lift that takes people up to the top of the mountain. Someone in the queue has their phone out.

Up the line, the chairs are coming down fast. Too fast. The lift had lost power and stopped. Then it started moving again, the wrong way, running backwards at about twice its normal speed, with people still sitting in the chairs.

At the bottom there is nowhere for them to go. The chairs swing into the station one after another and slam into each other. Some riders are thrown off. Others jump into the snow before they reach the wheel. The crowd in the queue scatters, then runs back in to help.

Nobody died. Eleven people were hurt, among them tourists from Sweden and Ukraine; a pregnant Swedish woman was flown to hospital in Tbilisi, and her injuries turned out not to be life-threatening. The lift had passed an inspection three months earlier.

A week later, Georgia's economy minister gave the result of an investigation by the testing company Bureau Veritas. The power cut had stopped the lift, but the rollback was human error: the operator should have switched to the backup diesel drive and brought everyone down safely, and he didn't follow the procedure. He was fired, two senior managers at the state company that ran the resort resigned, and police opened a criminal case. No court outcome was ever reported.

### 2. Stage under a black sky before the Indiana State Fair collapse (2011, Indianapolis, USA) — \~520 words

**Status / quick facts:** Saturday 13 August 2011, 8:46 p.m., Indiana State Fair grandstand, Indianapolis, minutes before a Sugarland concert. 7 dead (4 at the scene, 3 later in hospital), 58 injured. Cause per the Thornton Tomasetti engineering review for the State Fair Commission: the temporary roof rig (about 70,000 lb) was tied to loose concrete Jersey-barrier ballast that could resist only 25–43 mph winds; the gust was about 59 mph; code required 68 mph. Witt Associates found no formal protocol to delay or cancel a show and that the 8:39 p.m. National Weather Service warning never reached the decision-makers. Legal: IOSHA fines Feb 2012 (Mid-America Sound $63,000, settled 2014 at $50,000; stagehands' union IATSE Local 30 about $11,300; State Fair Commission $6,300); $50M civil settlement in 2014 (state $11M, Live Nation, Sugarland and others $39M). No criminal charges found. Clip tier: AMBER (flag) — the filmer's own upload; this before-clip is not in the AP/ABC packages, but the same filmer's collapse clip went out through AP (RED, not used). Used by: S5 @18:24 · S13 @33:55 (2 niche channels: Abyssal Detective 2, Stories From John).

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Abyssal Detective 2 "the Most Disturbing Videos Before Disaster [Vol. 6] Iceberg" @18:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7UjUZw3VluA&t=1104s) — segment ~18:24 · [Reference only — not footage: Stories From John "20 Most Disturbing Videos Before Disasters Ever Caught On Camera" @33:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwlgeJ9N6eM&t=2035s) — segment ~33:55

**Summary**

On the evening of 13 August 2011, the grandstand at the Indiana State Fair was packed for the country duo Sugarland. The show was due to start at 8:45. Fans had pushed right up to the front of the stage. Above them hung a temporary roof: a steel truss loaded with lights and speakers, about 70,000 pounds, standing on towers and tied down with straps to concrete road barriers.

To the west, the sky was turning black. At 8 p.m. the fair's executive director, Cindy Hoye, wanted to delay the show. Sugarland's side preferred to go on a few minutes late, and she agreed. Around 8:30, a state police captain, Brad Weaver, recommended cancelling and getting an evacuation plan ready. At 8:39, the National Weather Service warned of one-inch hail and winds over 60 mph. That warning never reached Hoye or Weaver.

At 8:45 a fan at the edge of the grandstand pressed record. The stage stands lit under a sky that's almost black. Then she swings the camera east, over the parking lot, and you can see the storm front: a hard shelf of cloud rolling in over the car lights. An announcer told the crowd there were clouds to the west and where to go if they had to leave, but that everyone was hoping the weather would pass, and Sugarland would be out in a couple of minutes. The crowd cheered. Almost everyone stayed.

Hoye and Weaver were walking to the stage to order an evacuation when, at 8:46, a gust of about 59 mph hit. The rig tipped forward and came down on the fans at the front. Four people died there, three more in hospital, and 58 were injured.

The engineers found the rig was never strong enough. The barriers it was tied to simply slid; they could hold back only 25 to 43 mph of wind, when the code demanded 68. A second review found nobody had a clear rule for calling off a show. The state's safety agency fined the stage company, the stagehands' union and the fair commission. In 2014 the families and the injured settled for $50 million; the state's $11 million share was only possible because lawmakers added $6 million on top of a legal cap. Nobody was charged with a crime. Indiana brought in its first rules for inspecting outdoor stages in 2012, but they were temporary, and they were later allowed to lapse.

### 3. Whakaari / White Island tour before the eruption (2019, Bay of Plenty, New Zealand) — \~570 words

**Status / quick facts:** Monday 9 December 2019, 2:11 p.m., Whakaari / White Island, an active volcano about 50 km off the Bay of Plenty coast. A steam-driven eruption sent a plume about 3.7 km high. 47 people were on the island (38 from the cruise ship Ovation of the Seas): 22 died, two of them never found; 25 survived with injuries, all but three severe or critical (mostly burns). GeoNet had raised the alert level from 1 to 2 on 18 November 2019. Legal: WorkSafe NZ charged 13 parties (30 Nov 2020). Six pleaded guilty — White Island Tours, Volcanic Air, Kahu, Aerius, Inflite and GNS Science; NEMA's charge was dismissed, the Buttle brothers' individual charges and two other firms' charges dismissed. Island owner Whakaari Management Ltd convicted at trial 31 Oct 2023. Sentencing 1 Mar 2024: about NZ$10.21M reparations and about NZ$2M fines in total. On 28 Feb 2025 the High Court (Justice Simon Moore) overturned WML's conviction. A coroner's inquiry held hearings in Oct 2025 (no finding found). Clip tier: AMBER — civilian GoPro clip that RNZ and NZ Herald aired with credit; the filmer deleted his own upload; clean credited repost; no agency holds it (Michael Schade's clips are Storyful = RED, not used). Used by: S5 @30:13 · S13 @42:43 (2 niche channels: Abyssal Detective 2, Stories From John).
