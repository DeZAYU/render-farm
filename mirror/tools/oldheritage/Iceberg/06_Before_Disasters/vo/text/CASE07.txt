Edouard Bozon is a skier from Lyon, and in the spring of 2020 he was high above Chamonix with a partner and a helmet camera running. They'd roped down a steep rock section, and now they were skiing a narrow couloir off the Capucin glacier with no rope between them.

At the bottom of the couloir, there's a deep crack where the snow meets the glacier, called a bergschrund. Edouard stops right beside it to wait, while his partner's still on the slope above him.

His partner had shifted his weight up there, and a small slab of snow broke loose and swept Edouard over the edge. He's wedged inside with his skis jammed and a small hole of daylight overhead. And what's one of the first things he says down there? Warn the others, because anyone still coming down could knock more snow in on him.

His partner found the hole fast, but he didn't have a rope. He lowered a strap, then his ice axe. Edouard was scared that unclipping his skis would drop him deeper, but he finally got both off. He pulled himself up far enough for his partner to haul him out.

A rescue helicopter flew him off the mountain with a badly hurt knee. The next March, he posted a new film of himself skiing steep lines again.
