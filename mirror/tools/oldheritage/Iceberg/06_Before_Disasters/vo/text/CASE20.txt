On November 17, 2015, in the morning rush on Cape Town's N2 highway, traffic officer Nizaam Alexander pulled over a silver Mercedes that was driving in the yellow emergency lane. His patrol car's dashcam kept rolling. A city safety official said lots of their officers run cameras because drivers lie about what happened.

He's at the driver's window doing the paperwork. While he's there, check the grass verge on the left of the frame.

The man in the dark hoodie had come up behind him and tried to take his service weapon, and when Alexander reacted, he was shot in the back. His vest stopped anything serious. Alexander rolled clear and chased the attacker on foot toward the shacks beside the highway, but the man got away into the informal settlement.

The Western Cape Government released the footage in January 2016 and asked the public for help. Officials said people recognized a man from that grainy footage, and a suspect was arrested in early February 2016. No trial or verdict has ever been made public.

What people remember, though, is what Alexander did that morning. He walked back to the Mercedes and finished issuing the fine for the yellow lane. He didn't go for medical help until he was done. You can see the hole in the back of his uniform while he writes.

Who does that? Someone who'd just won the City's departmental Traffic Officer of the Year award for 2015. He got that award before this morning, not because of it.
