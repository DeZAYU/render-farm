A student pilot lines up for runway 10 at North Perry Airport in Pembroke Pines, Florida, on a clear Tuesday morning, November 12, 2019. He's flying solo in a Cessna 172, with a camera clipped behind the seats watching over his shoulder.

The plane was built in 1978, so it was 41 years old that day. For about twenty seconds the approach looks textbook, with his hands steady on the yoke and the runway numbers sliding closer. He'd later tell investigators his airspeed was slightly high.

Keep an eye on the nose right after the wheels touch.

Right rudder and right aileron didn't stop the swing to the left. The plane ran off into the grass and across a taxiway. It hit a taxiway sign before it tipped onto its left side.

He climbed out on his own with minor injuries at most, and he later went back to training. Fire crews cleaned up a small fuel leak, and the runway was closed for a while.

With a plane that old, I'd have guessed something broke. But he reported no mechanical failure, and the National Transportation Safety Board's report doesn't point to one either. His flight school said he should've aborted the landing and gone around. The board's probable cause was the student's failure to keep directional control during the landing.
