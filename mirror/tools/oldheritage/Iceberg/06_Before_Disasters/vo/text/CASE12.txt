Vaasa Arena is the ice hall in Vaasa, on Finland's west coast, and on December 17, 2017, about 3,000 people packed into it for Raskasta Joulua, or Heavy Christmas. That's a touring show where metal singers belt out Christmas carols. Sonata Arctica's Tony Kakko was singing, and a fan up in the stands was filming the stage.

The show had already used fake snow and other effects. So when the stage pyro died away after the third song, most people thought nothing of the rattling that started up in the roof, right above the stage.

That was a piece of the hall's exhaust air duct, at least three feet wide, coming down with a lighting rail. Kakko first thought it was another effect, then shouted "Watch out" into the mic. It's about 480 pounds of metal, and it dropped more than 30 feet into the standing crowd, though electrical cables slowed it down.

There was no stampede. One witness said nobody was panicking, and everyone was just bewildered. A doctor or nurse in the crowd started first aid before the ambulances got there. Investigators said about twenty people were hurt and nobody died, but they warned that the pieces coming down edge first could've killed someone.

Finland's Safety Investigation Authority found the duct hadn't been hung properly. One of its brackets had already failed a few days earlier, and that put the extra weight on the other hangers until they couldn't hold it.

Five men were convicted of negligent bodily injury, including the CEO of the company that'd modified and fastened the duct. By the end of 2021, the Vaasa Court of Appeal had upheld their verdicts.
