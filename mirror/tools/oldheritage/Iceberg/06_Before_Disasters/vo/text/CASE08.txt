This clip was shot from the floor of Santika, a famous club on Ekkamai in Bangkok, at 11:44 p.m. on December 31, 2008. The man holding the camera is Scott Garpot, an American who'd come with his brother. The party's called Goodbye Santika, the last New Year's blowout before the club moves to a new home, and about a thousand guests and staff are packed inside.

On stage, a band called Burn is playing, and at the front of the stage, fountains of sparks shoot up in time with the music. Then Garpot tilts up to the balcony, and they're shoulder to shoulder up there, waiting for a countdown that's about fifteen minutes away.

Nobody in that room knew what the building was on paper. Santika wasn't licensed as a nightclub at all. Officials later found it'd been registered as a food business that had to close at midnight, and as a private home. It hadn't once been inspected for fire. Even the city architect's signature on its plans turned out to be forged.

So how many ways out did a thousand people have? One main front door, about eight feet wide, plus a staff exit. A third exit had been locked to keep thieves out. There was one fire extinguisher.

Sometime after midnight on January 1, 2009, the ceiling above the stage caught fire. Garpot wrote that it'd spread across the ceiling in less than four minutes. Then the lights went out and part of the ceiling dome came down. Hundreds of people pushed toward the one door they knew, and it jammed solid.

Sixty-six people died and 222 were hurt, with citizens of 13 countries among them. Garpot's brother was one of the injured, and he was still in intensive care when Garpot posted this clip two days later. Garpot wrote that he'd dropped his own camera while helping other people get out.

Police first pointed at the singer and the stage effects. The Justice Ministry's forensic experts later cleared him and said a stage effect wired into the electrics was the likely source. The cause hasn't ever been officially settled. All anyone can say for sure is that it started on that stage.

In 2011 the owner and the pyrotechnics contractor got three years each for negligence. After an appeal went the other way, Thailand's Supreme Court gave the owner three years again in 2015. Another court ruled that Bangkok's city government shared the blame for letting the place stay open.

The spot on Ekkamai where Santika stood has since been rebuilt as a lifestyle mall.
