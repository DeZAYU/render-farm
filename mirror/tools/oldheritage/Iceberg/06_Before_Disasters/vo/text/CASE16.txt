The hut in this video is a turf-roofed shelter that Tyson Peers built by hand for his bushcraft channel, in the woods near his home in Hawkesbury, Ontario. On the afternoon of Saturday, May 21, 2022, he was hauling rocks for its chimney, and his brother and a friend were helping out.

Then his phone goes off. The screen reads 4:27, and it's an emergency alert for a dangerous thunderstorm. The other two want to run for the house. Tyson tells them there isn't time, and that out in the open, with trees all around, is the worst place they could be. He gets everyone into the hut and holds onto them.

From here the camera's inside in the dark, and the doorway's your only window.

Two trees came down right on top of the hut, and the roof held. It's all over in minutes, and when they crawl out, the path home's buried under snapped trunks. Tyson said afterward that staying put "turned out to be the best decision because we would have died." Looking at that path, I don't doubt it for a second.

What hit them was a derecho, a fast-moving line of storms. This one ran about 600 miles across Ontario and Quebec in roughly nine hours, and its strongest measured gust, in London, Ontario, was about 103 miles an hour. More than 1.1 million customers lost power, and it's one of the costliest natural disasters Canada's ever had. At least 12 people were killed, most of them by falling trees.
