Allessandro Kauffmann had come to New Zealand from Brazil with his partner, and on Monday, December 9, 2019, he wore a GoPro for a day trip to Whakaari, also called White Island. It's an active volcano about 30 miles off the Bay of Plenty coast, and for years tour boats had been taking visitors right onto the crater floor. Many of the people heading out that day had come ashore from the cruise ship Ovation of the Seas.

Three weeks earlier, the volcano's official alert level had gone up from 1 to 2. Tremor and gas readings were the highest they'd been since 2016. The tours didn't stop.

At first it's just a vacation video, with the wharf and the boat ride out to the island. The group goes ashore in an inflatable and everyone's handed a hard hat. Then they're walking over gray rock and yellow sulfur crust, and the steam's pouring out of vents all around them while the guide points things out.

They pass the rusted ruins of an old sulfur works and head back down to the jetty. Kauffmann's group was among the last to tour the crater that day. They climbed back onto the boat, and he said later it wasn't even five minutes from then until the volcano erupted. At 2:11 in the afternoon, a column of black ash and steam shot up out of the crater behind them, about 2.3 miles high. On the boat, a guide came over the loudspeaker and told everyone to move inside.

There'd been 47 people on the island. Boats nearby turned back and put inflatables in the water to pull people off the jetty, and three helicopter pilots landed in the ash and flew 12 survivors out. Twenty-two people died, and two of them were never found. The other 25 survived, almost all of them badly hurt.

WorkSafe New Zealand, the country's workplace safety regulator, later charged 13 parties over the tours. Six pleaded guilty, including the tour company and GNS Science, the state-owned institute that ran the volcano monitoring. The island's owners, Whakaari Management, fought their charge and were convicted after a trial in 2023. In March 2024, the court ordered more than 10 million New Zealand dollars in reparations, roughly 250,000 for each survivor and each bereaved family.

Then, in February 2025, the High Court overturned the owners' conviction. Justice Simon Moore ruled that they hadn't controlled the tour workplace, so the company that owned the island doesn't carry a conviction for any of it. Later that year a coroner began hearing evidence, and the families' lawyer told him that many of them still believe they should never have been walking in that crater.
