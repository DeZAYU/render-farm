On the morning of March 17, 2021, people in Nizhnevartovsk started calling the police about a bear on the street. It's an oil city in western Siberia with about 280,000 people, and the bears are supposed to stay in the forest outside it.

This one was a young female brown bear. Locals said she'd been wandering the city's industrial edge for about an hour from around 9 a.m., frightened and confused. One driver caught her walking along a roadside snowbank by a red and white building, with two stray dogs trailing behind. The car keeps rolling closer.

She passed within a few feet of that side window. That's a bear at rush hour, right on the other side of the glass.

Across another road, past a crash barrier and a blue striped warehouse, a second phone found her sitting in a snowbank and rolling around in it. A man's standing a few yards to her right, by a little white booth, talking on his phone. For about half a minute neither of them goes anywhere.

That rush toward him was only the start. He ran, and she chased him along the sidewalk past a car dealership. Drivers pulled people off the sidewalk and into their cars. When he dashed across the road, a city bus came through, and the bear ran straight into its path. Some reports say the bus hit her, and others say it braked just short. Either way, the man got away, and police said no one was hurt.

So where does a bear in a Siberian city come from? She came from a hotel, not the forest. Its owner, a local businessman, had bought her and her mother from a traveling circus in December 2018 to advertise the place, and the two of them lived in a cage on the hotel grounds. A hotel kept two bears as advertising, and now one of them was loose on a city road.

Police and Rosgvardia, Russia's national guard, ringed her with cars and closed the only road to the airport. She squeezed into a gap between a snowdrift and a heating main. The police chief, Fanis Khamatov, said they were waiting for the vets so they wouldn't have to destroy her, and could sedate her and move her somewhere safe instead. In the end, welders from a nearby plant reportedly built a corridor on the spot to steer her into a transport cage.

Then the owner reportedly told police he was fine with her being put down. A police spokeswoman said he didn't care at all about the animal's future, and that police definitely weren't going to do it. The mayor, Vasily Tikhonov, said he'd been flooded with appeals and had questions for the owner who'd put residents at risk.

That same day, police helped specialists move her into an enclosure. Six days later they announced they'd found no offense of any kind. I think that's the most disturbing part of the whole story. Both bears went back to the man who'd bought them from the circus.
