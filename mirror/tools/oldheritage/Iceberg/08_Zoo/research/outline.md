### 1. The Hat in the Tiger Yard (2016, Toronto, Canada, Toronto Zoo) — \~500 words

**Status / quick facts:** Saturday 16 April 2016, just after 3 p.m. (the filmer's description says 3:17 p.m.; CityNews via HuffPost says "just after 3 p.m."). Place: the Sumatran tiger exhibit at the Toronto Zoo. Animal: Hari, a 7-year-old Sumatran tiger (IBTimes UK, quoting the zoo). People: an unnamed woman in a red top, never identified; the filmer is visitor Jared Sales; zoo spokeswoman Jennifer Tracey. What happened: her hat ended up past the visitor barrier; she climbed over the outer barrier into the gap in front of the tiger's inner fence, the tiger sprang at the fence, and she grabbed the hat and climbed back out. Injuries: none. Outcome: the zoo only learned of it hours later, from the video. It opened an investigation and asked witnesses to come forward. CityNews (via Newser) reported she could face a fine of up to $2,000 and a long-term ban if identified. No report says she was ever identified, fined or banned, and no police were involved. The tiger was not harmed. Tier: **AMBER** (zoo.md: "his own upload. CBC, Inside Edition and ITV aired it with credit. No agency hits."). Niche use: 3 reference videos from 2 channels (Top Fives, plus MostAmazingTop10 twice). The raw clip itself has 3.9M views, so viewers may recognise it.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Top Fives "Top 15 STUPID ZOO GUESTS" segment](https://youtu.be/11lGkvSKo1s?t=73) — segment \~1:13 · [Reference only — not footage: MostAmazingTop10 "Disturbing Things Caught on Camera Inside Zoos" segment](https://youtu.be/TxtMy2jriBI?t=808) — segment \~13:28 · [Reference only — not footage: MostAmazingTop10 "Disturbing Zoo Camera Footage No One Can Explain" segment](https://youtu.be/EuHK5Jo0W-M?t=597) — segment \~9:57

**Summary**

It was a sunny Saturday afternoon in April 2016, and the Toronto Zoo was busy. Along the boardwalk above the Sumatran tiger yard, families leaned on the rail to watch Hari, a seven-year-old Sumatran tiger, pace his enclosure. Somewhere along that rail a woman in a red top lost her hat. It landed on the wrong side of the barrier.

The exhibit has two lines of defence. First comes the barrier visitors lean on. Behind it is a narrow gap, and then a second, taller fence that actually holds the tiger. The woman climbed over the first one and dropped into the gap.

A visitor named Jared Sales was filming through the mesh from across the yard. His clip opens with her already down there, a red dot against the far fence. Hari has noticed her. The tiger comes across the yard and launches at the fence right below her, then rears up again. Only the wire is between them. She grabs the hat and starts climbing back up. The tiger paces under her the whole way, and within about ten seconds she's back over the rail and gone.

The crowd didn't take it well. People on the boardwalk can be heard calling her a moron and telling her she was a bad example for everyone's kids. One voice wonders if she risked it all for a five-dollar hat.

Nobody from the zoo saw it happen. The keeper watching the tigers was at another pen, and staff only found out hours later, once the video was online. By Monday it had more than a million views and was being shown on news sites around the world. Spokeswoman Jennifer Tracey called it "very irresponsible". She said the inner fence meant the woman would never have touched the tiger, but that she could easily have broken an ankle jumping down. She also said Hari was just showing "instinctive behaviour". Her advice was simple: if you drop something, tell staff, because they have the equipment to get it back.

The zoo opened an investigation and asked witnesses to come forward. Under its by-laws she faced up to a $2,000 fine and a long ban. She was never named. As far as any report shows, she walked out of the zoo with her hat, and nobody ever found out who she was.

### 2. Shamba Turns on the Man Who Raised Him (2018, Thabazimbi, Limpopo, South Africa, Marakele Predator Centre) — \~700 words

**Status / quick facts:** Date: Saturday 28 April 2018, about 11:20 a.m., per the family's statement (TimesLive, Sowetan). Conflicting: The Citizen gives Monday 30 April, and a police spokesman, Lt Col Moatshe Ngoepe, is reported as saying "Monday" (secondhand, not seen directly). Place: Marakele Predator Centre, also called Marakele Animal Sanctuary or Predator Park, a private facility just outside Thabazimbi, Limpopo. SANParks stressed it is about 2 km from Marakele National Park and not part of it. Animal: Shamba, a 10-year-old male lion, hand-raised and bottle-fed by the family from birth. Person: owner Mike (Michael) Hodge, a British expat, 72 per the family (67 in the Sun / NZ Herald). Injuries: a broken jaw, neck wounds and several lacerations. He was stabilised at a local hospital, airlifted to Milpark Hospital in Johannesburg, and walked again with a physiotherapist days later. **He survived.** The lion was **shot dead** during the rescue (verified across TimesLive, Sowetan, OFM, HuffPost UK). Who fired is disputed: a staff member outside the camp (News24, via HuffPost UK and CTV), or a tourist with a revolver, told to shoot by Hodge's daughter (The Citizen). Aftermath: the park closed, then reopened for business in May 2018 (News24, 18 May 2018, headline). The NSPCA called the killing unnecessary, and the Endangered Wildlife Trust called for the facilities to close. No charges were reported. Tier: **AMBER** (zoo.md: "a tourist's clip, aired by CBS, Fox and the Daily Mail. No agency hits. Filmer not traceable. Graphic: trim before the drag."). Niche use: 2 channels (Occult Inquisitor, Obscure Encounters).

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Occult Inquisitor "The Most Disturbing Safari Incidents Ever Recorded" segment](https://youtu.be/fEnXiHybYBY?t=3233) — segment \~53:53 · [Reference only — not footage: Obscure Encounters "25 Most Disturbing Animal Encounters When Wild Animals Get Way Too Close" segment](https://youtu.be/_xM7RgchGEM?t=1046) — segment \~17:26

**Summary**

Mike Hodge had known Shamba since the day the lion was born. He and his wife Chrissy had left Britain years earlier and built a small predator park outside Thabazimbi, in the bush country of Limpopo. They opened it to the public in 2010. Shamba was the star. The family bottle-fed him as a cub, and ten years later he was a full-grown male, close to the people who raised him.

Visitors toured the lion camp in a caged truck. The park advertised a "lion mobile" where a lion would jump up and look you in the eyes. There was a routine at the exit. Hodge would get out to open the gate, and a staff member outside would distract Shamba and draw him away.

On a Saturday morning in late April 2018, around 11:20, Hodge was showing a group round. A tourist was filming on a phone. The clip shows Shamba walking past a small hut by the gate in the bright sun. Then the lion is out in the long grass, and Hodge, a grey-haired man in a grey shirt, walks across the camp toward him.

Shamba turns. He charges, catches Hodge and drags him away from the gate into the bushes, while the people in the truck scream for help. Then shots ring out. The lion lets go and runs, and a man shouts for someone to get a rifle.

The family said a staff member had moved Shamba away as usual, but the lion "mysteriously turned around". A friend told the Sun that Hodge had gone in with a ranger because a smell was unsettling one of the lions.

Shamba was shot dead. Reports disagree on who fired. News24 said a staff member outside the camp shot him to save Hodge's life. The Citizen said a tourist had a revolver, fired into the air first, and then shot the lion when Hodge's daughter told him to.

Hodge, 72, survived with a broken jaw, neck wounds and deep cuts. He was airlifted to a hospital in Johannesburg. His wounds were left open to fight infection, and within days he was walking again. His family said he was traumatised and heartbroken over Shamba, and called it a "double tragedy."

The clip spread around the world. Animal-welfare groups said it showed exactly why wild animals shouldn't be kept for tourists. South Africa had around 8,000 lions in captivity. Within weeks the park was open again.

**Summary**

South Africa's drive-through lion parks have one rule, printed on signs at every gate: keep your windows closed and your doors locked. In early 2014 two American sisters were visiting their parents, who were in South Africa on a church mission, and the family drove into one of these parks. Their phone was rolling.

The clip opens calmly. A pride lies in the grass a few metres from the road: tawny lions in front, white lions stretched out behind. Then one young lion gets up and walks straight toward the car. It brushes past the window so close that its eye fills the frame.

It circles to the back of the car. Through the rear passenger window you see its face looking in. Then it bites down on the plastic door handle and pulls, and the rear door swings open.

The phone tumbles. There is screaming. Someone grabs the door, slams it and presses the lock. In the scramble you can hear the line every news report later quoted: "You were supposed to lock the door." The lion stays a moment, then goes back to the pride lying beside the road.

Nobody was hurt. The sisters' brother, Joshua Sutherland, posted the clip on 12 March 2014 under a plain title, "Lion opens car door." For almost a year almost nobody watched it. In March 2015 it was posted on Reddit, and within 48 hours it had about 1.4 million views. Within days it was on Inside Edition and news sites around the world.

South Africa's best-known lion handler, Kevin Richardson, said he wasn't surprised. He called it "a curious young male habituated lion" and "a tempting piece of plastic", and said lions explore with their teeth because they don't have fingers. The park was never named, and it never commented.
