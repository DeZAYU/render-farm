## How the cases were chosen

**Method (Ekam's approved caught-on-camera method, Nov 12):** the cases are exactly the MAIN LIST of training/caught-on-camera/incidents.md, in its order, most proven first. That list takes the cases that the long-running channels in our niche already used (mainly Obscure Encounters, plus Chilling Scares Compilations, Abyssal Detective 2 and Chezzoid). For each case it finds the same clip the reference video showed and traces the earliest upload of that footage. No own-research cases, and the list's Backup section is not used.

**Cold-open note for the script:** open cold on case 1's camera, with no preamble scene from another case. April 2025, a ridge above Whitewater, California: a helicopter crew's camera finds a lone Pacific Crest Trail hiker frozen on a crumbling cliff face, and a rescuer is lowered on a cable toward her. Hold one beat, then go wide: every clip in this video was filmed by someone who was there. That means climbers, kayakers, anglers, skiers, a paratrooper, campers and rescue crews, and none of them knew what the next minute held. Promise the run: cliffs, open water, ice, wild animals and other people, ending in a motorhome car park in the middle of the night. Keep it under 150 words (it's a 30-minute video). Don't name overdone anchors (Elisa Lam, Lars Mittank, the Manila hostage bus).

### 1. The PCT hiker frozen on a crumbling cliff, and the hoist "bear-hug" rescue (2025, Whitewater area, Riverside County, California, filmed by the Rescue 9 aircrew) — \~270 words

**Status / quick facts:** Sunday 13 April 2025, Whitewater area of the Pacific Crest Trail, north of I-10, just south of the San Bernardino County line. A female PCT thru-hiker (name not released) went off-trail where the route branches. She ended up on a steep, crumbling slope above a drop and held that one spot for over an hour with a heavy pack and almost no footing. She sent an emergency text to 911 from a Garmin-type satellite device. The Riverside County Sheriff's Office Aviation Unit sent helicopter Rescue 9. Rescue specialist Jason Beeman was lowered on the hoist. Asking her to let go and raise her arms for a rescue strap was judged too risky, so he bear-hugged her and the crew hoisted them both to the top of the cliff, then into the aircraft. Outcome: scrapes and bloody legs, no serious injury. She had been on the trail about 20 days and ended her hike. No charges. Filmed on the aircrew's helmet and aircraft cameras and first posted 14 Apr 2025 on Hook-in-Hand, the Rescue 9 aircrew's YouTube channel. RCSO also posted about it on Instagram. From the approved list: #1, tier AMBER; used by Obscure Encounters "9 Most Disturbing Incidents Ever Caught on Camera" @ \~1:42 and Obscure Encounters "25 Most Disturbing Incidents Ever Caught on Camera That Will Haunt You" @ \~12:45. Freshness: the original has about 1.76M views and was covered widely by US TV (ABC7, CBS LA, FOX LA) in April 2025. It's well known but recent.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Obscure Encounters "9 Most Disturbing Incidents Ever Caught on Camera", segment](https://youtu.be/E-Ddr5-oQHo?t=102) — segment \~1:42

**Summary**

It's spring on the Pacific Crest Trail, and thru-hikers are pushing north out of the desert toward the San Gorgonio Pass. Near Whitewater, just north of Interstate 10, the trail splits into several branches. One woman, about twenty days into her hike, takes the wrong one. The path runs out on a slope of grey, crumbling rock above a drop, and when she tries to move, the ground won't hold her. So she stops and grips the cliff. She has a big pack on her back, her red trail shoes are braced on almost nothing, and she can't go up or down. She manages one thing: an emergency text to 911 from her satellite messenger. Then she waits, holding the same position for more than an hour.

The Riverside County Sheriff's helicopter Rescue 9 finds her as a tiny figure on a pale face of loose rock. The helmet camera rides down the hoist cable with the rescue specialist. As he lands beside her, her legs are shaking. The crew can't ask her to let go and lift her arms into a strap, because one slip could pull her off the face. So he wraps his arms around her, pack and all, and tells her to hold on to him. The helicopter takes their weight and swings them off the rock, up toward the ridge. On firm ground she collapses onto her side, then sits up and drinks water while the crew checks her scraped, bloody legs. A second hoist lifts her into the aircraft. She walked away with only cuts. Her hike was over, but she was alive.

**What the source video adds**

- It claims "unstable weather" and "strong gusts repeatedly pushed both of them toward the rock face". Corrected: on the clip's own radio a crew member says "Doesn't look like there's any wind." No report mentions wind. Drop the gusts.
- It says "a hoist line nearly 200 ft long". The radio calls out "195, 200 now" during the approach, but no report confirms the cable length. Say "far below the helicopter" instead.
- It says she "had become lost in hazardous terrain". That fits: the crew's description says she "got off trail due to the complexity of that section as it branches off".
- Obscure Encounters (25 Most…) says she "slipped down a cliff near the summit" and was trapped "for hours". Corrected: she was not near any summit, it was a desert section of the PCT. Official accounts say "over an hour" and "almost an hour", not hours.

### 2. The lobster diver who lost the way out of an underwater cave (2013, Southern California coast, filmed by the diver, Kodiak Thunder) — \~190 words

**Status / quick facts:** The dive happened in or before December 2013 at a spot off Southern California that the diver calls his "honeyhole" (the exact location is not given). He was diving with his father. In his words, they "pulled 2 nice limits" of lobster, including "a couple 6 pounders", plus a 6-pound cabezon. Chasing "a big bug" (a lobster) into a cave, he "got lost at one point and nearly drowned", then got out unhurt. There was no rescue, no injuries and no official record. Everything comes from the diver's own upload. He filmed it on his own torch-lit POV camera and posted it 13 Dec 2013 on his channel Kodiak Thunder (Instagram @spearfish_socal). From the approved list: #2, tier GREEN; used by Chilling Scares Compilations "2 Hours of the Most Disturbing Moments Caught on Camera" @ 1:39:24 and "2 Hours of the Most Disturbing Caught on Camera Videos" @ 28:27. Freshness: the original has about 13K views and no press coverage. It's only been seen through the Chilling Scares compilations.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Chilling Scares Compilations "2 Hours of the Most Disturbing Moments Caught on Camera", segment](https://youtu.be/AfRqFHdcSVY?t=5964) — segment \~1:39:24

**Summary**

It's lobster season in Southern California, and a young diver and his father go out to the spot he calls his honeyhole. California spiny lobsters hide in rock crevices and come out at night, and divers here are only allowed to take them by hand. That means reaching into the dark. The camera on his head sees only what his torch beam hits: a tunnel of white light, rock walls covered in growth, and orange antennae twitching deep inside the cracks. A lobster that feels threatened doesn't run. It backs deeper into the rock and braces against the walls, so the diver has to follow it in.

It's a good night. Lobster after lobster gets pulled from the rock and stuffed into a yellow mesh bag. Then he spots a big one, a "big bug", and follows it further in. The rock closes around him. He reaches, grabs, and wins the fight with the lobster. But when he turns around, every direction looks the same. The beam swings across walls, back the way he thinks he came, and finds nothing but more rock. He later wrote that he got lost in there and nearly drowned. Underwater, a few wrong turns in the dark burn through the one thing you can't get back: air.

Somehow he finds the way out. The footage ends almost casually. His father's light appears in the black water, the bag is full, and he comes up with two limits of lobster and a six-pound cabezon. He posted it all with three exclamation marks: "Thankfully I got out fine!!!"

**What the source video adds**

- Chilling Scares dates the upload right ("On December 13, 2013, YouTuber Kodiak Thunder uploaded…") and correctly says he caught the lobster before realising he was lost.
- It claims "after swimming around in the cave for a few minutes, he was able to find the exit". The diver gives no duration ("got lost at one point"), so "a few minutes" is unconfirmed. Don't state a time.
- It says he catches fish and crustaceans "by hand or with a spear gun". Correct as far as it goes: lobster has to be taken by hand in California. Divers may carry a speargun, but can't use it to take lobster on the same dive (CDFW).
- It then spends about a minute on silt-outs and cave-diving guidelines. That's generic. Nothing in the upload mentions silt, a guideline or gas mixtures.
- It never names the place, and neither does the diver: only "SoCal" from his handle. Don't name a location.

**Extra material for the script**

- Upload 13 Dec 2013, titled "Huge Lobster and Near death experience in cave!!" [Kodiak Thunder upload].
- He dived with his father, and they "pulled 2 nice limits with a couple 6 pounders" plus a 6-lb cabezon [Kodiak Thunder description].
- His own words: "Chased a big bug into a cave and got lost at one point and nearly drowned. Thankfully I got out fine!!!" [Kodiak Thunder description].
- California spiny lobsters are active at night and hide in crevices by day, backing deep into the rock and bracing against the walls when threatened [Wikipedia: California spiny lobster].
- A crevice can hold one lobster or a group of 2 to 15 [Wikipedia: California spiny lobster].
- Males can reach 7.4 kg (16 lb). The record is a 16 lb 1 oz male caught off Catalina Island in 1968 [Wikipedia: California spiny lobster].
- Under California rules divers may take lobster only with their hands: no spears, no tickle sticks [CDFW spiny lobster page].
- The daily bag limit is seven lobsters, so "two limits" means about 14 between him and his dad. The minimum size is 3¼ in carapace [CDFW spiny lobster page, current rules].
- The season runs from early October to mid-March, so a December dive is in the middle of it [CDFW spiny lobster page, current rules].
