A climber in his forties was alone on Mount Futago, west of Tokyo, on October 1, 2022, and it was his third time on the route. Just before nine, he was down-climbing the rocky ridge below the west peak, with long drops on both sides and a GoPro on his head. His bear bell rattles nonstop on rock, so he'd silenced it for the descent.

About 160 feet below the summit, he heard heavy, animal snorting from above. He turned around.

A full-grown black bear was coming straight down the rock at him. Where do you go on a ridge like that? Nowhere, because one wrong step means a fall. He twisted away from the first lunge, and the bear slid past him, then scrambled back up and came at him again from below.

He said that instead of fear, he switched to the feeling that if it was coming, he had no choice but to face it.

He screamed the whole time and brought his fist down on it like a hammer. Then came a front kick, and about 20 seconds after it started, the bear crashed off into the bushes below. I don't think I'd have kept my footing up there, let alone hit back. Going back through the footage, he decided it'd been a mother with a cub nearby.

He says he came away with scraped, cut hands from gripping the rock and a slightly sprained wrist, both minor. Rather than carry on down toward where the bear had gone, he climbed back up to the summit, took a breather and went down the way he'd come.
