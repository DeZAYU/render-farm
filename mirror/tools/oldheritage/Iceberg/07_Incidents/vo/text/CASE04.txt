The Grand Couloir du Goûter is a steep gully on the most popular route up Mont Blanc, the highest peak in the Alps, and climbers call it the Couloir of Death. Almost everyone on that route crosses it twice, going up and coming down. It's only about 330 feet across, and the only protection is a steel cable strung over it, under a face that sheds rocks all day long. So how often does that face actually drop something?

In the summer of 2019, sensors in the couloir picked up more than 2,600 rockfalls in 68 days. That's about 39 a day.

In July 2020, Katrin Merisalu's team was on the way down from the summit, waiting at the edge of the gully for their turn to cross. Her phone was recording. Out in the middle, on a band of ice and snow, one climber in dark clothes was crossing alone.

Then the mountain lets go.

This time, Merisalu wrote, it all ended well. Between 1990 and 2011, accidents on this stretch of the route killed 74 people, so I doubt that's a sentence anyone gets to write very often. The climber was rescued later by helicopter, and that's about all anyone knows.
