On March 30, 2017, just after seven in the evening, a 24-foot sailboat left King Harbor in Redondo Beach, California, with four people aboard, all in life jackets. The wind was howling, and the sea was white with chop.

They couldn't get back behind the breakwater. Every gust shoved the boat sideways toward the pier, where people were gathering at the railing, and one of them, Jason Wells, stopped his electric unicycle and started filming.

From up there, it's easy to see where this ends long before the crew can do anything about it. They're fighting the sail as the boat slides in under the outer pilings, and for a second, it looks like they'll scrape past.

Then a bigger wave stands up behind them.

The impact shook the pier, Wells said, and he was almost on top of it. Now there are four heads in the white water between the wooden pilings and the rocks, and everyone at the railing is yelling at them to swim for shore.

One by one, the sailors made it in to the rocks, where lifeguards and firefighters were arriving. The captain and another man went to the hospital with a dislocated shoulder and back pain, and the other two were treated on the beach for cuts. Fire Division Chief Rob Rappaport said they were lucky to be alive, and I don't think anyone on that pier would argue. As for the boat, it broke apart on those pilings, and it didn't even turn up in the first search.
