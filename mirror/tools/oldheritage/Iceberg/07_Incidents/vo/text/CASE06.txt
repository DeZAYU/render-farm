Ted Engelhardt, a creek paddler from around Erie, had wanted to run Belson Creek for years. It's a tiny snowmelt creek in a deep gorge near Ripley, New York, and he'd once camped down there alone for four days to learn it and clear fallen trees. That was four days in a gorge for one run, and I'm not sure I would have had the patience.

On March 14, 2015, the creek was high with meltwater. Ted and his friend Frank put on at 4:30 in the afternoon, with nobody on the bank holding a rope. It was getting late, so Ted scouted Gage's Falls in a hurry.

The red bow tips over the lip.

The boat lands in the boil and stalls. The hole rolls him for close to a minute, and about halfway through he's out of the kayak. His GoPro sat about 11 inches above his mouth, so the camera is often above water when he isn't. How can you tell when he's under? He wrote that if you can't hear him moaning, he probably is.

A deep pool saved him. He balled up and sank, and the current flushed him downstream. After that, the camera catches his bare hands on the gorge wall.

He swam another 300 feet, then the pair hiked a mile and a half through knee deep snow in the dark. His empty boat circled in that hole for over 20 minutes. The accident report he filed himself logs his injury as hypothermia. His lesson afterward was never to run it with fewer than three people.
