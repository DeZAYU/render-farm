The Bassmaster Elite tournament on Lake Champlain was running a day late, because a storm had torn across the lake the day before. It was August 10, 2024, and Wes Logan, a pro from Springville, Alabama, had four bass in the boat and wanted a fifth.

Around a quarter to nine, he was running across the middle of the lake at about 50 miles an hour, his deck camera rolling. The sky's clear now, but the water's still choppy from the storm.

For about fifteen seconds, it's just the bow and the spray.

"I don't really know what happened," he said later. The best guess is a piece of dock, ripped loose by the storm and floating just below the waterline, though nobody's confirmed it. Whatever it was, it tore the engine off the back of the boat, and a safety leash kept it from flipping in on top of him. An ambulance met him at the shore. He needed stitches to his face, and his ribs were cracked.

I'm pretty sure most people would've called it a day right there. Wes Logan got clear scans after three hours in the hospital and went right back out on the water. He caught his fifth bass, about three pounds, and climbed from around 85th place to 60th.
