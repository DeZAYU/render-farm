Picture a pile of bleached driftwood on the point of a big, gray lake, one camera on a tripod and another on a hunter's beanie. That's where Ethan Gooch set up in November 2020, after a three quarter mile walk in the dark to hunt alone.

He was in waders, waterproof overalls with the boots built in. They keep you dry until water gets over the top.

A group of four mallards dropped in. He shot one, but it fell well out in the water, and with no boat, he waded out. For about 30 yards it stayed chest deep, and he had no idea there was a drop-off.

The next step just isn't there.

His waders filled with lake water. I'm amazed he didn't panic. He swam back toward shore until his boots could touch. And the duck? He grabbed it on the way.

Back on the driftwood, he called it a boneheaded move, going after the bird too fast. Once your waders fill, he said, you sink fast, and being out there by yourself is a whole other deal.
