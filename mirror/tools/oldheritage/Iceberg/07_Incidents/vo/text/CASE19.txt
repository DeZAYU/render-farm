On January 2, 2019, five people from a property firm walked into a row house in Philadelphia's Angora neighborhood that'd been empty for at least three years. They buy run-down houses to fix up and sell, so a walkthrough like this was routine.

One of the men started up the stairs, and a co-worker stopped him. The steps were covered in broken glass, and a thin line ran across one of them.

Ekrem Uysaler started filming. The line ran up the stairwell to the ceiling, where an old crutch hung upside down with a big knife on the end. Would it work? They weren't going to find out with a foot. One of the men stood back and hooked the line with a long pole. Watch the top of the stairwell.

The crutch swung down across the stairs, and the blade stopped right at neck height for a grown man. Nobody was hurt. I can't stop thinking about the guy who almost went up first. He was about to walk right into it, and he's only fine because somebody looked down.

Uysaler called whoever built it a genius. A colleague posted the clip as a warning to other investors, and a Facebook repost reached about 12 million views. As for the firm, it didn't walk away. It went ahead with the renovation anyway.

Nobody's ever come forward, and nobody knows who that line was strung for.
