In Almaty, Kazakhstan, the Ulken Almatinka river reaches Lake Sairan through a steep channel of smooth concrete. In late May 2016, fast brown floodwater was tearing along the foot of that wall, and a small black dog was stuck in the corner where the wall meets the water.

A young man climbed down and reached it. But he couldn't climb that steep, smooth wall back up holding a dog, and now there were two of them trapped at the waterline. A woman named Marianna was filming from the far bank.

Up at the railing, strangers start lowering themselves down, hand in hand.

One of the men, Birzhan Seidakhmet, said that first chain just couldn't reach. So more people came off the railing, and Seidakhmet, who says he was the tallest, was lowered further to close the gap.

This time it's four or five men long, wrists locked, with the top man anchored to the fence.

The man and the dog were hauled up hand over hand, and nobody got hurt. Marianna posted it with one line: the world isn't without kind people. At the time, nobody knew who those men were, and what I love is that none of them had to go over that fence. In March 2026, a sculpture of the chain called Unity was unveiled on the Sairan embankment, and the last figure's hand reaches past the railing for anyone to take.

We're not even halfway yet, and before this list is over, a man alone on a knife-edge ridge will have to fight off a bear with his fists.
