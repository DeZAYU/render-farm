Gijón sits on the north coast of Spain, and above its San Lorenzo beach runs a long promenade on top of a high sea wall. On Sunday, February 2, 2014, a huge swell storm was hitting that whole coast, with waves of up to 11 meters, about 36 feet, and it landed on the spring high tides.

It's like half the town came down to watch. Hundreds of people stood shoulder to shoulder along the railing, filming the breakers, while a local named Manfredo Alvarez filmed them from across the road.

The sea below is pure white, and every wave throws spray up over the crowd, but nobody in that line steps back. I'd have been three blocks away.

Then one wave doesn't stop at the wall.

People were still running between the parked cars as the next waves hit the wall behind them.

No serious injuries were reported in Gijón that day, which I honestly didn't expect. Later that month, Spain approved nearly 44 million euros to repair what those two days of storms did to the north coast, and 265,000 euros of it went to San Lorenzo itself. When the next big storm rolled in that March, police put up a perimeter on the promenade to keep the crowds back.
