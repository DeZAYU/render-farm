On June 3, 2017, at around three in the afternoon, Mather Point was packed. It's the busiest viewpoint on the Grand Canyon's South Rim, and a visitor named Cyndi was there for her birthday weekend, filming over the railing.

Then the crowd started calling out. A barefoot man in his twenties had climbed over the guard rail and sat down on a rock ledge a few feet below it, with the canyon dropping away behind him. By Cyndi's account, he'd been drinking.

I keep coming back to how Cyndi put it afterward. Past that rail, she said, you're on your own responsibility. Everyone keeps telling him to come back up, and he keeps saying he will, but he doesn't move.

They ask him one more time.

He didn't come back up. Seconds later he lost his grip and went over. Somebody shouted not to look, and someone called 911. A helicopter was over the rim in about five minutes, Cyndi wrote, but reaching him took hours.

This is the helicopter that came for him.

Three responders were lowered down the cliff to stabilize him. Then he was short-hauled out, hanging on a rope under the helicopter, and taken to Flagstaff Medical Center. So did he make it? For weeks, Cyndi had no idea. Then word reached her through his family that he was alive, with some brain injury. The park put his total fall at about 100 feet, and he survived it.
