Kim and Bill are a married couple who film their fishing trips, and in June 2019 they paddled two yellow kayaks through the surf at Matagorda, Texas. It was only Kim's second day ever fishing beyond the breakers, out past the waves near an offshore rig, in a north wind of 15 to 18 miles an hour.

Kim hooked something on a chunk of jack. It didn't run. It just pulled, steadily, away from shore. Bill paddled over and they held their kayaks together, so now it towed both.

After about 30 minutes Kim's arms were done, and Bill took the rod while the rig kept shrinking behind them. Whatever's on the other end has now dragged two loaded kayaks a long way from where they started, and it still hasn't shown itself.

Then a fin breaks the surface.

It's a bull shark, one of the three species most likely to bite people, and they put it at close to ten feet. Kim didn't want to be anywhere near it, and on her second day out past the breakers, I can't blame her. Bill got it beside his kayak again and again, until the knot gave out, probably at the swivel. By their count the fight ran about an hour and 15 minutes, and it had dragged them more than a mile off the rig.
