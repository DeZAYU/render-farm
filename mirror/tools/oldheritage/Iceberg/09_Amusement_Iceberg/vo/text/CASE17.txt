Rod Chayko and David Sager were the last two to board Mindbender on June 14, 1986, so the friends got the back car. In front of them sat an engaged couple, Tony Mandrusiak and Cindy Sims. The ride had been briefly shut that evening, then reopened.

Mindbender ran inside West Edmonton Mall in Canada, the biggest shopping mall anywhere in the mid-eighties. It had opened in December 1985 as the tallest indoor coaster in the world, about 145 feet of steel with three vertical loops.

After the big drop, heading for the third loop, the back car started to fishtail. Its wheel assembly had come apart. Chayko said his lap bar just let go, and there wasn't anything else holding them in.

The train didn't have the speed for the last loop. It rolled backwards, and the wrecked car slammed into a concrete pillar. Somewhere in all that, the four riders in the back car were thrown out.

Tony and Cindy didn't survive, and neither did David. Chayko spent more than six months in the hospital and had to learn to walk again. He didn't find out his best friend was dead until three days later.

A provincial inquiry found the bolts holding the wheel assembly had worked loose and fallen out. How many bolts were holding it on? Four. I honestly expected a bigger number than that. The inquiry didn't blame the mall. It blamed design and manufacturing faults by the West German builder.

Mindbender came back in 1987 with shorter trains and seat belts, and it ran for another 35 years before it closed for good on January 30, 2023. Chayko spent decades asking for a memorial at the site, and there's never been one.
