On Sunday, June 9, 1991, Kings Island in Ohio had the worst night in its history. Two unrelated accidents happened about 75 minutes apart.

At around 8:30, a 22-year-old named Timothy was crossing a wooden walkway over the lake. He reached down to splash the water, a jolt went through him, and he fell in.

His friend Eddie Haithcoat jumped in after him. So did Darrell Robertson, a 20-year-old park security guard. I don't know that I'd have been that brave.

But the water was live.

A pump that kept the lake moving had surged, and it didn't have working ground-fault protection, the safety switch meant to cut the power the instant electricity leaks where it shouldn't. Eddie and Darrell were both killed. Timothy, the man they'd gone in to save, survived with burns.

And the night wasn't over. At about 9:45, across the park, Candy Taylor, 32, from Toledo, rode Flight Commander, where riders steered two-seat pods with a joystick and could roll them all the way over.

Candy was alone in her pod. At some point she slipped out of her restraint into the empty seat beside her, which had nothing holding it closed. When the pod went upside down, she fell from the pod, and she died in the hospital.

The coroner found she'd been drinking, but officials blamed a design flaw. A ride that flips shouldn't have an open seat you can slide into.

OSHA fined Kings Island $23,500 over safety violations at the lake. Flight Commander came back in 1992 with seat dividers and new restraints, and it was gone after 1995.

Three people died in one night, and fans of the park still call it Black Sunday.
