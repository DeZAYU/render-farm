# The Scary & Disturbing Amusement Parks Iceberg Explained

## How the cases were chosen

**Method (Ekam's list of 13 Nov):** the cases are exactly the 20 in training/caught-on-camera/amusement-iceberg.md, in its 5 levels and order. The spares weren't used. Every case comes from an established niche channel's iceberg or compilation (Abyssal Detective and Abyssal Detective 2, MonoMania, Occult Inquisitor, Obscure Encounters, LUMI Moments, TCK Moment, REALITY WATCH). Each entry carries its reference video and timestamp as "Reference only — not footage". **20 cases, all from reference videos, 0 own research.**

**Pool:** the list frame-checked every reference iceberg at 5-second steps (training/…/work/theme-parks/framecheck_K1–K4.md). Those icebergs almost never show genuine raw incident clips for cases we can take. They narrate over stock, photos and TV. So this iceberg uses the exact raw reference clip wherever one is GREEN or AMBER (7 cases), and narrates the other 13 over stock, Creative Commons or public-domain photos and public records, as the references do (minus their TV and agency material).

**Earlier-videos exclusion — about 100 park cases out:** every case on Ekam's channel (batches/old-heritage-files/exclusions.md) is out, including Big Thunder Mountain, America Sings, Space Mountain and Top Thrill Dragster. None of the 20 is on it. The ledger's fuzzy match flagged two false hits: Dueling Dragons ("Islands of Adventure", matched to his Ripsaw Falls fire) and Ocean Park ("Halloween", matched to a horror-franchise mention). Both were claimed under clearer names.

**Ledger:** all 20 are claimed for this title and none is held by another title. Released: Tibidabo (child at the centre under this list's filter), Kora-kora and the Facilpa dog rescue (spares, not used).

**Content filters:** no death shown on camera, no child at the centre, no graphic injury detail. Every victim's age was checked: all the people killed or centrally hurt are adults (Mindbender 21–24, survivor 25; Black Sunday 20–32; Inferno 18; Columbia 33; Ride of Steel 29; Texas Giant 52; Haunted Hotel 22 (not 23 as the reference says); Perilous Plunge 40; Ocean Park 21; Parc Saint-Paul 35 and 32; Dueling Dragons 52). Minors appear only among many injured, at Son of Beast, Mindbender and Green Plaza. They stay unnamed and unshown. **Green Plaza: children were among the injured; none is the subject of the footage.**

**Fiction / hoax cases:** none. El Gusanito (#3) is a real clip whose place and date can't be confirmed, and the entry says so.

**Levels:**
- **Level 1, The Tip (1–7):** filmed malfunctions and famous near-misses (the slingshot cord, the log flume, the worm coaster, the Futuroscope fire, the deer, the lost eye, Son of Beast).
- **Level 2, Below the Surface (8–12):** overseas collapses on camera, then the first deaths (Queens Land, Albanifest, Green Plaza, the Columbia cleat, Inferno).
- **Level 3, Dark Waters (13–15):** riders who should never have been let on, or restraints that didn't hold (Ride of Steel, the Texas Giant, the Haunted Hotel).
- **Level 4, The Abyss (16–17):** design failures that killed (Perilous Plunge, Mindbender).
- **Level 5, The Deepest (18–20):** a park's deadliest day, a death backstage in a horror maze, and a ride that killed twice and kept running.

**Left out, and why:**
- **Child at the centre:** Tibidabo 2010, the Haunted Castle fire 1984, Battersea 1972, Terminal Velocity, Superman Tower of Power, Lagoon "Puff", Matterhorn 1964, Verrückt, Greeneville, River Country, Lane Graves and Mission: Space.
- **RED footage:** Shaoyang (Newsflare), the Ninja (TV helicopter) and the Small World fire (watermarked).
- **Ekam's channel:** every case in the exclusions file.

**Tone:** factual and restrained. Each death is stated once, plainly, and never shown. Charges and convictions are described exactly as they were. Wherever a reference video got the facts wrong, the entry corrects it.

**Footage rule:**
1. The exact raw reference clip, GREEN or AMBER, with in/out times taken from the frames (cases 1–4 and 8–10).
2. Stock b-roll from Pexels, Pixabay or Storyblocks.
3. Wikimedia Commons, CC or public-domain photos: real files, each credited exactly as its file page gives it.
4. Public records (OSHA, state and court records, the Alberta inquiry) as document crops or text cards.

Never TV footage, agency photos or news photos. **Channel rule waived:** under Ekam's risk-tier method, the old small-channel rule doesn't apply to approved reference clips. Each link-check FAIL that's on that rule alone is marked in its row.

**Length:** about 30 minutes, planned at **6,030 words**: the clip cases at 260–340 words, the narrated cases at 250–350. Each target is what the verified facts honestly carry. Nothing is padded.

**Footage note for writers:**
- **Raw clips are short:** Queens Land is one 11-second take, the log flume is 14 seconds, and Green Plaza's moment is 3 seconds of wide CCTV.
- **Commons coverage is thin for some cases:** there's no Commons photo of Flight Commander, the Kings Island pond, Ocean Park's maze or Parc Saint-Paul's Formule 1. Those cases lean on park photos, stock and text cards from the records.
- **Records:** most official records (OSHA, Cal/OSHA, state reports, court rulings) aren't online. Script them as reading the record, citing the agency.

## Level 1 — The Tip

### 1. The Vertical Accelerator cord that shredded on the launch pad (2019, Cobra Adventure Park, Panama City Beach, Florida, USA) — \~300 words

**Status / quick facts:** Saturday night, 17 Aug 2019 (WMBB: "On Saturday night", story dated Wed 21 Aug; the Facebook video went up Sun 18 Aug). Ride: the "Vertical Accelerator", a slingshot-style ride with a two-seat steel cage launched by rubber bungee cords strung from two towers, on Front Beach Road, Panama City Beach. Two adult men strapped in; one of the two main cords frayed and shredded as the operator was about to release them, so the cage never left the deck. No injuries. The riders were taken off and refunded. The park kept the ride open. The Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS), which inspects rides at Florida parks this size, said it would inspect the ride on Thursday night (22 Aug) and check the cords' maintenance and number of uses. I found no published result of that check, no fine and no closure. Freshness: biggest YouTube video found: "Slingshot ride cable breaks seconds before aerial launch l ABC News" (ABC News), 2.18M views (YouTube search, Nov 2026). Next: the News Herald clip (305k) and WMBB News 13 (230k), so 1 result ≥500k. Under the old ENTRY_BRIEF rule one English video ≥1M would mean FAILS FRESHNESS. It's on Ekam's main list, so it stays, but the lead should know. Covered in two of the reference videos: LUMI Moments (ESdHkEMZo08) and TCK Moment (QFSK-y-ESsM).

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: Moments Filmed Seconds Before Disaster | Craziest Amusement Park Accidents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESdHkEMZo08&t=60) — segment \~1:00–1:20, cord snaps at \~1:07, on-screen "Credit: krissy Hurst" and "No one was injured" (LUMI Moments · 13.7k subs · upload 2026-06-30). Same clip in TCK Moment [QFSK-y-ESsM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QFSK-y-ESsM&t=27) at \~0:27–0:38 (TCK Moment · 57.1k subs · upload 2026-06-06).

**Summary**

It's a Saturday night in August 2019 on Panama City Beach's Front Beach Road. At Cobra Adventure Park, two men are strapped into a yellow steel cage called the Vertical Accelerator. Two giant rubber bungee cords run from the cage up to two towers. The cage is held down to a wooden deck while the cords stretch tight, and then it's released and flung into the night sky.

Krissy Hurst, from Havana, Florida, has ridden it many times. Tonight she's filming instead, because the ride's own camera system isn't working. Her friend backed out at the last second with "a bad feeling." In the cage are that friend's acquaintance and his brother-in-law, who is visiting from California. It's their first time. A woman off camera teases that they'll be "screaming like sissies."

The attendant in the yellow shirt goes to the launch lever. Then the left cord bursts. In a few seconds it frays into a cloud of white strands, and the cage jolts on its mount with the riders' hands still up in the air. It never leaves the ground. "We just dodged death," a woman says.

Nobody was hurt. The men were taken off and given a refund. Hurst said she couldn't stop laughing at their faces, but nobody at the park really seemed sorry.

The video went viral within days. The park manager said they run safety checks every day, and the ride stayed open. The state agency that inspects Florida's rides said it would look at whether the failed cord had been maintained properly and had gone past its allowed number of uses. If it failed within its limit, the agency would go to the manufacturer. No outcome was ever made public.

**What the source video adds**

- LUMI Moments (0:59–1:20) and TCK Moment (\~0:27–0:38) play this exact clip with only a credit and a "No one was injured" caption. Neither names the park, the ride or the date, and neither says anything about the aftermath. Everything above is own research.
- Correction (LUMI): the "Credit: Panama City News Herald" caption LUMI puts on the clip just before this one (an Indian spinning ride) actually belongs to this Florida clip.
- Correction (headlines): many outlets (ABC, Fox) say "cable". It was a rubber bungee cord. The operator of the separate Slingshot-brand rides stressed that its own rides use industrial steel cables, "rather than rubber cables" (WMBB, Aug 2019).
- Correction (press): Tampa Bay Times spelled the filmer "Krissy Hare" and showed a "Krissy Gwin" credit. The News Herald, CBS Miami, UPI and ClickOrlando all name her as Krissy Hurst.
- Don't use ABC's line that rides like this fling riders "300 feet into the air at 100 miles per hour". That was a general description, with no ride specification behind it.
- Not the "Slingshot" brand: the Vertical Accelerator and Cobra Adventure Park are not affiliated with Slingshot Group of Companies, which runs the Funtime-trademarked Slingshot rides in Orlando, Kissimmee, Daytona Beach and Panama City Beach (Slingshot Group statement to WMBB).

**Extra material for the script**

- The incident happened on Saturday night, 17 Aug 2019. Hurst posted her video on Facebook the next day ("Watch till the end.. In Panama City Beach @ The Cobra on Front Beach Road..") and emailed the link to the Panama City News Herald (WMBB, 21 Aug 2019; Tampa Bay Times, Aug 2019; News Herald YouTube description).
- Hurst had ridden it many times. The ride's video system wasn't running, so she and a friend filmed it themselves. The friend decided not to ride at the last moment because of a "bad feeling" (Fox 35 / FOX TV Stations, 21 Aug 2019).
- The riders were the friend's acquaintance and his brother-in-law, who was visiting from California. It was their first time on the ride (Fox 35, Aug 2019; UPI, Aug 2019).
- The riders later went on Good Morning America under their first names only, Nick and Vic. "I was totally in shock at the moment the cable snapped… my hands still stuck in the air not knowing what happened" (ABC News, 22 Aug 2019; WMBB, Aug 2019).

- The state's statement on what it was checking: "We are collecting information on the cords that failed to ensure the operator was within regulation regarding maintenance as well as the allowable number of uses. If the cord failed within the acceptable number of uses, we will contact the manufacturer and investigate further." The department said it would be on site Thursday night to inspect (Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services statement to WMBB News 13, Aug 2019).
- WMBB had first asked FDACS for comment and got no reply. Its update carried the statement above (WMBB, 21–22 Aug 2019).
- FDACS's Bureau of Fair Rides Inspection is responsible for rides everywhere in Florida except the big parks, those with more than 1,000 employees and their own full-time inspectors. A park like Cobra falls under the state ([FDACS Fair Rides Inspection](https://fairrides.fdacs.gov/); Florida House bill analysis HB 1275, 2020).
- No published FDACS finding, fine or closure order could be found. Say "the results were never made public", not that the ride was cleared (own search, Nov 2026).
- The Slingshot Group partner said, by contrast, that its Slingshot rides use "industrial steel cables rather than rubber cables" and a patented Funtime "Spring Machine" (Slingshot Group statement, WMBB / ClickOrlando, Aug 2019).

### 2. Saw Mill Log Flume (2021, Six Flags Great Adventure, Jackson, New Jersey, USA) — \~300 words

**Status / quick facts:** Sunday 13 June 2021, about 6:30 pm. Saw Mill Log Flume: an Arrow Development log flume that opened with the park's Frontier area on 4 July 1974. It is about 2,160 ft long, has a four-storey lift and runs 5-seat log boats. A guide rail on the bend near the station was loose or broken. The boat ahead (carrying the filmer's daughters and nieces) jolted against it. The next boat struck the wall and was left tipped on an angle (about 45° by the witness's estimate) without overturning. 2 riders were taken to hospital (one with leg pain, one with arm pain). First-aid staff helped the others in the boat. No serious injuries were reported. The park closed the ride for inspection by its own safety and engineering teams. It reopened by mid-July 2021 after the broken guide rail was repaired. No DCA finding about the flume has been published (see below). No charges, no fine and no lawsuit found. Freshness: biggest YouTube video found: "Video shows moments before Six Flags log flume accident" (6abc Philadelphia), 1,293,944 views (YouTube search, Nov 2026). 1 result ≥500k: it is the same Thomas clip in a TV wrapper, not an explainer. In 1 of the 20 reference transcripts: Obscure Encounters NrqycPU0T68 @ 25:42.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: 20 Most DISTURBING Theme Park Incidents Ever Caught on Camera](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NrqycPU0T68&t=1540) — Saw Mill Log Flume segment \~25:42–26:53 (this NJ.com clip at 25:45–26:00, then narration over news/aftermath shots) (Obscure Encounters · 15.8k subs · upload 2026-10-29)

**Summary**

It's a grey Sunday evening in June at Six Flags Great Adventure. The Saw Mill Log Flume has been carrying riders since the park's first summer in 1974. A father from Long Island is waiting near the end of the course while his daughters and nieces ride. His wife is filming. Their log comes down the last drop, swings around the bend toward the station and gives the girls what he calls "quite a jolt". Nobody notices why until later, when they watch the video back: a section of the guide rail on that bend is out of place.

Less than a minute later, the next boat reaches the same spot. It slams into the wall, rides up and stops, tipped over at roughly forty-five degrees in the water channel. A woman in the front is crying. Park first-aid staff wade in to help everyone out, and two riders are taken to hospital, one with leg pain and one with arm pain. Six Flags says the boat "did not complete its cycle" and closes the ride for "a thorough inspection".

The father's clip, published by NJ.com two days later, became the story. It shows the loose rail moments before the crash, on a ride Six Flags was still advertising as the world's first log flume. "It definitely could have been my children," he said. "They were probably less than a minute away from that happening to them."

About a month later the flume was running again with the guide rail repaired. In 2024 it shut for a long refurbishment and reopened in 2026, its 52nd year.

**What the source video adds**

- The OE segment (25:42–26:53) uses this exact clip (OE 25:45–26:00 = our clip 0:02–0:12) plus a news/aftermath shot.
- OE says the guide rail "was not inspected or addressed in time" and calls it "a technical fault that could have been identified beforehand". No inspection finding says that. Six Flags never gave a cause, and no DCA report on the flume has been published. Say "the rail was already broken minutes before the crash", not "they ignored it".
- OE says "the first boat … slipped off the track, hit the side, and tilted completely to one side", then "another boat … collided, creating a chain of impacts". That's wrong. The FIRST boat (the Thomas girls) only jolted against the rail. The SECOND boat hit the wall and tipped partway; Six Flags said it tipped "on an angle" and did not overturn. No boat-on-boat collision was reported.
- OE's "operating for nearly 50 years" is fine (47 years in 2021).
- Correct the popular claim: Six Flags' own website (quoted by the Inquirer and Inside Edition) called it the "world's first ever log flume ride". The first log flume was El Aserradero at Six Flags Over Texas (1963), also built by Arrow. Don't repeat "world's first".
- The filmer: 6abc says Steven Thomas was NOT on the ride and his wife filmed. The Jet Set says he was on a boat with them. Go with 6abc (direct interview): he watched from outside, his wife filmed, and the girls were in the boat ahead of the injured riders.
- Fox News/USA Today's "aftermath video" was filmed by a different guest (Noel Brown), and Fox said it was "unknown if [the rail] was like this before or after". NJ.com's clip settles it: the rail was already broken BEFORE the injured boat came through (NJ.com caption, 15 Jun 2021).

**Extra material for the script**

- Time and injuries: about 6:30 pm. Two guests were hospitalised, one with leg pain and one with arm pain. Spokesperson Kristin Fitzgerald would not say how many were in the boat (Philadelphia Inquirer, 13 Jun 2021).
- Six Flags' statement: "The safety of our guests and employees is our highest priority" (Inquirer; PhillyVoice; ABC7NY, Jun 2021).
- Six Flags said the boat "did not complete its cycle" and stopped at about the point shown in the broken-rail video. The ride "is currently undergoing a thorough inspection by our safety and engineering teams" (Inside Edition, 18 Jun 2021).
- The park later added "will remain closed until further notice … Until that work is completed, we will not speculate" (Fox News, 17 Jun 2021).
- NJ.com's caption: "A broken and out-of-place guide rail is seen on the bend just before the ride station … Moments after the video was shot, a boat that followed struck the wall, tipped slightly and left two people hurt" (NJ.com YouTube description, 15 Jun 2021).
- Thomas: his girls "got quite a jolt"; "I didn't see it hit the wall but saw it just after. A woman in the front appeared to be crying"; the flume stopped about 10–15 ft past the broken rail (iHeart/DC101, 16 Jun 2021).
- Thomas: the boat was "just leaning on an angle … It's not like they were going to fall out. It honestly didn't look that bad," and he'd ride it again (The Jet Set, 16 Jun 2021). Use this for an honest, non-hyped tone.
- First-aid staff assisted "several guests who had been in the boat" (Jersey Shore Online; ABC7NY, Jun 2021). Wikipedia notes "a boat nearly tipped sideways" (Wikipedia: Incidents at Six Flags parks).
- Ride facts: Arrow Development, 2,160 ft, 27 boats of 5 adults or 900 lb each, highest point about 40 ft. Opened 4 July 1974 (1994 park fact sheet reproduced on greatadventurehistory.com; Wikipedia: Six Flags Great Adventure).
- State oversight: NJ's Department of Community Affairs (Carnival-Amusement Ride Safety Unit) licenses and inspects every ride in the state (nj.gov DCA). No DCA statement, inspection result or fine about the flume was published or reported. Every 2021 news story credits only the park's own inspection. Say "no state finding was ever made public", not that the state cleared it.
- Reopening: NJ 101.5 reported on 16 Jul 2021 that the Log Flume "is running again after repairs were made to a broken guide rail". A park vlogger rode it on 14 Jul 2021 (Coliwood Studios vlog, uploaded 17 Jul 2021). That means it was closed about a month.
- Two weeks later, on 29 Jun 2021, El Toro partly derailed at the same park. The DCA "red-tagged" it pending a manufacturer report. PhillyVoice framed it as the park's second incident in two weeks (PhillyVoice, 1 Jul 2021). This is good bridging context: summer 2021 was a bad run for the park.
- Afterwards: the flume closed for a long refurbishment in 2024 (Coliwood Studios "Log Flume Update 3/30/24") and was promoted as "returning in 2026" (WOBM, 9 Feb 2026). It was open on 16 May 2026 (Coliwood Studios vlog). Six Flags' own 2026 POV: "52 years young … This year, the ride received a major refurbishment" (Six Flags Great Adventure YouTube, 14 Jul 2026). The 2024 refurbishment is not linked to the 2021 crash in any source; don't imply it was.

**Handle with care:** Minor injuries only ("leg pain", "arm pain"). Don't inflate them to "seriously hurt", and don't say "flipped" or "capsized". It tipped and did not overturn. The injured riders are not named and not shown. The riders in the clip are Thomas's daughters and nieces (ages not published). They weren't hurt, but they are young girls whose faces are visible at 0:05–0:08.4, so keep that stretch out or blur it. The cause is not officially established. "Broken guide rail" is what the video shows and what NJ.com and NJ 101.5 report. Don't say Six Flags or the state found negligence. No charges, fine or lawsuit found.

### 7. Son of Beast: a cracked timber, 27 riders to hospital, then closure (2006–2009, Kings Island, Mason, Ohio, USA) — \~350 words

**Status / quick facts:** Son of Beast was a wooden hypercoaster that opened on 28 April 2000. Its designer of record was the Roller Coaster Company of Ohio (an affiliate of RCCA). It stood 218 ft, with a 214 ft drop, a top speed of 78.4 mph, 7,032 ft of track and a 118 ft vertical loop. At opening it was the tallest and fastest wooden coaster in the world and the only looping one. At about 4:45 pm on Sunday 9 July 2006, a structural failure in the "Rose Bowl" helix made a dip in the track. The passing train jolted, and all **27 riders were taken to hospital**, most with minor chest and neck injuries. 17 were released within five hours, and nobody had life-threatening injuries. In December 2006 the Ohio Department of Agriculture (ODA) found a design flaw: the wooden supports were not designed for the ride's weight, and a vertical support called a "bent leg" cracked. The loop was removed and lighter trains fitted, and the ride reopened on 4 July 2007. It closed for good on 16 June 2009 after a rider, a **39-year-old woman (adult)**, reported a burst blood vessel in her brain. The ODA found no irregularities, but the ride never reopened. It was demolished in 2012 (the lift hill came down on 20 November) and replaced by Banshee in 2014. Five lawsuits followed the 2006 accident, and there were no criminal charges. Minors: no ages were published for the 2006 riders. Any minors were among many injured and unnamed, so they don't trigger the child-at-centre rule. **Summary**

In April 2000, Kings Island opened a wooden coaster nobody had dared to build. Son of Beast was 218 feet tall, dropped 214 feet, hit 78 miles an hour and turned riders upside down in a 118-foot loop. It was the tallest, fastest and only looping wooden coaster on Earth.

### 16. "Perilous Plunge" fall (2001, Knott's Berry Farm, Buena Park, California, USA) — \~270 words

**Status / quick facts:** 21 September 2001. Perilous Plunge was an Intamin "Mega Splash" shoot-the-chute: 121 ft tall with a 115 ft drop at about 78°, billed at opening as the tallest and steepest water ride in the world. It opened on 15 Sep 2000, so the death came almost exactly a year later. One rider died: Lori Mason-Larez, 40, of Duarte, California, a mother of five. Adult; her children were on the ride, but none was hurt, named or at the centre. The ride closed after the death. In March 2002 Cal/OSHA found the restraints "inadequate" and a contributing factor, and ordered changes before reopening; the boats were retrofitted with four-point harnesses. The family sued Cedar Fair and Intamin (outcome not found). No criminal charges. The ride closed for good on 3 Sep 2012.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: the Most Disturbing Theme Park Incidents Iceberg](https://youtu.be/SeYxN0N-_bI?t=2802) — segment \~46:42

**Summary**

In September 2000, Knott's Berry Farm opened Perilous Plunge. Its boats dropped 115 feet at close to 78 degrees, just 34 feet short of Niagara Falls. Knott's billed it as the tallest, steepest water ride on Earth.

A year later, on 21 September 2001, Lori Mason-Larez, a 40-year-old mother of five from Duarte, rode it with her family. Her niece and several of her children sat beside her. Her lap bar and seat belt were in place. During the descent she came out of the boat, and she was killed.

The ride shut, and the argument began. The manufacturer, Intamin, pointed to the coroner's report: her waist was about eight inches larger than the boat's 50-inch seat belt. Besides, the ride's only posted rule was that riders be at least four feet tall. Nobody had ever been told there was a size limit.

In March 2002, after a six-month investigation, California's Division of Occupational Safety and Health sided largely with the family. It ruled the restraints inadequate and said they had contributed to her death. Knott's had to either change the restraints or restrict who could ride. The boats came back with four-point harnesses. Perilous Plunge ran for another decade before it was torn out in 2012.

### 17. The Mindbender derailment (1986, Fantasyland, now Galaxyland, at West Edmonton Mall, Edmonton, Canada) — \~320 words

**Status / quick facts:** Saturday 14 June 1986, evening. Mindbender: an indoor triple-loop steel coaster by Anton Schwarzkopf (designer Werner Stengel), opened 20 Dec 1985 at a cost of $6M. It was the tallest indoor coaster in the world (44.2 m). The last car of a four-car train lost a wheel assembly before the third loop. Its lap bars released and all four riders in it were thrown out. 3 dead: David Sager, 24; Tony Mandrusiak, 24; his fiancée Cindy Sims, 21. 1 critically injured: Rod (Rodney) Chayko, 25. 19 people taken to hospital. Cause (Alberta Board of Inquiry, Oct 1986): four cap screws holding the bogie wheel assembly came loose. The board blamed design and manufacturing faults by the West German maker and also criticised maintenance and inspection, without linking those directly to the crash. The ride was closed for more than a year and came back with three-car trains, extra wheel assemblies, anti-rollback devices, seat belts and headrests. It ran until 30 Jan 2023 and has since been removed. **Minors check: PASS.** All three dead and the survivor were adults (21-25). Two teenage riders from Stettler were in the train that hung upside down. They were among the people hurt, not at the centre: keep them unnamed.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: the Most Disturbing Theme Park Incidents Iceberg](https://youtu.be/SeYxN0N-_bI?t=4401) — segment \~73:21

**Summary**

In 1986 the world's biggest mall had the world's biggest indoor roller coaster: the Mindbender, fourteen storeys of track and three vertical loops, twisting over a concrete floor in the mall's amusement park, then called Fantasyland.

On the evening of 14 June, the ride had been briefly shut down, then reopened. Two friends from Alberta, Rod Chayko, 25, and David Sager, 24, who were visiting the mall for the first time, were the last to board. They took the back car, behind an engaged couple, Tony Mandrusiak and Cindy Sims. A concert was playing below.

After the big drop, heading for the final loop, the back car started to fishtail. Its wheel assembly had come apart. The car swung across the track, the lap bars, the only restraint, unlocked, and all four riders were thrown out. The train didn't have the speed to clear the last loop. It rolled backwards, and the wrecked car slammed into a concrete pillar, leaving riders hanging upside down.

Three people died. Chayko survived with injuries so severe he spent more than six months in hospital and had to learn to walk again. He learned his best friend was dead three days later.

A provincial inquiry found four bolts had worked loose. It blamed design and manufacturing faults by the German builder. The Mindbender reopened with shorter trains, seat belts and anti-rollback brakes, and ran for another 35 years. Chayko spent decades asking for a memorial at the site. None was ever built.

**Extra material for the script**

### 18. Kings Island's "Black Sunday": the Oktoberfest pond electrocutions and the Flight Commander fall (1991, Kings Island, Mason, Ohio, USA) — \~330 words

**Status / quick facts:** Sunday 9 June 1991. Two unrelated incidents about 75 minutes apart left 3 dead and 1 hospitalised. (2) About 9:45 pm on Flight Commander, Candy Taylor, 32, of Toledo, fell about 60 ft. Kings Island had been open almost twenty years. On one Sunday night in June 1991, it had the worst evening in its history.

### 19. Ocean Park's "Buried Alive" haunted house death (2017, Ocean Park, Hong Kong, China) — \~260 words

**Status / quick facts:** Saturday **16 September 2017** (not October), around 2 pm, during Ocean Park's Halloween Fest. "Buried Alive" was a solo walk-through: each guest lay in a coffin whose bottom dropped away onto a slide down from the upper floor into a dark maze. Cheung Chiu-kit, 21, a Chinese University of Hong Kong first-year student, didn't come out. Staff checking a machinery stoppage found him unconscious in a staff-only mechanical area behind the ride. He had been hit by the moving coffin/slide mechanism. He was certified dead at Ruttonjee Hospital. The EMSD (government ride regulator) found no mechanical failure. The government ordered the attraction suspended, and it never reopened. A coroner later ruled the death an accident (\~2019). His family filed a negligence suit against the park in the High Court (2018); no outcome found. It was the first death at the park's Halloween event, which began in 2001. **Minors check: PASS.** The victim was 21.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: the Most Terrifying Theme Parks Incidents Iceberg](https://youtu.be/s-90Age6C1A?t=1346) — segment \~22:26

**Summary**

Every autumn, Ocean Park turned into Hong Kong's biggest haunted house. In 2017 its newest scare promised guests the chance to "experience being buried alive alone, before fighting their way out of their dark and eerie grave."

You went in alone and lay down in a coffin. Then the bottom dropped away and you slid down into a pitch-dark maze.

On a Saturday afternoon in September, a 21-year-old university student went in and never came out. Somewhere in the dark he took a wrong turn, into the space behind the attraction where the coffin machinery moved. That area was meant for staff only. When the ride's machinery stopped, a staff member went back to check and found him unconscious. He had been hit by the moving coffin mechanism. He died in hospital.

Government inspectors found nothing had broken: the machine had worked exactly as designed. The question was how a guest got into it at all. Why wasn't that area locked? Was the warning sign too dim to see? An inquest later heard that the performers inside should have stopped him, but they either didn't see him or mistook him for a colleague. The coroner ruled it an accident. "Buried Alive" never reopened.

### 20. Parc Saint-Paul's "Formule 1": two women killed on one ride, 11 years apart (2009 and 2020, Parc Saint-Paul, Saint-Paul (Oise), France) — \~330 words

**Status / quick facts:** "Coaster Formule 1" was a 15 m steel coaster with tight hairpin turns and strong airtime, a wild-mouse-style layout built by Pax of Russia. It opened at Parc Saint-Paul (near Beauvais) in summer 2005. **Jul 2005:** within 3 weeks of opening, a brake failure made one car hit two stopped cars, and 11 people were slightly hurt. Brakes were replaced by Soquet, and owner Gilles Campion was struck off France's park association, SNELAC. **Sat 22 Aug 2009, \~5 pm:** a 35-year-old woman from the Aisne, riding with her husband, was thrown out on a turn, fell about 10 m and died. In April 2010 an expert blamed the victim's "inappropriate behaviour" and the park was cleared. Seat belts were then added to the lap bars. **Sat 4 Jul 2020:** Élodie Duval, 32, from the Eure (Normandy), was thrown out near the end of the ride and died. She was there for her son's 2nd birthday. Prosecutors found the 2009 belts had been removed just before the park's post-Covid reopening in early June 2020, and the first locking notch on every lap bar had been ground off. Neither change was reported for a new safety check (the last approved inspection was Nov 2019). Campion admitted both in custody. He was charged with involuntary homicide (Jul 2020) and tried at Beauvais criminal court (21 Oct 2025). Verdict, 18 Nov 2025 (Le Parisien headline date; the AFP/Brut copy is stamped 14 Sep 2026 and puts the civil-damages hearing on 16 Sep 2026, so confirm the date): 3 years' suspended prison, a €40,000 fine and a 5-year ban from working in amusement parks. The park was sold to Groupe Looping on 20 Mar 2025, and the ride was demolished on 29 Jan 2026. **Minors check: PASS.** The victims were 35 and 32. Children were present (her 2-year-old son in 2020; the 2009 victim's children, 7 and 8) but weren't hurt: keep them out of the visuals and unnamed.

**Source video:** [Reference only — not footage: the Most Terrifying Theme Parks Incidents Iceberg](https://youtu.be/s-90Age6C1A?t=1465) — segment \~24:25

**Summary**

Parc Saint-Paul is a small family park in the woods north of Paris. In 2005 it bought a compact coaster from a Russian factory and called it Formule 1. Three weeks after it opened, its brakes failed and eleven people were hurt.

In August 2009, a 35-year-old mother was thrown out on a turn and died, as her husband and two children looked on. An expert blamed her "inappropriate behaviour", the park was cleared, and seat belts were added to the lap bars. Formule 1 kept running.

On 4 July 2020, Élodie Duval, 32, came with her family to celebrate her little boy's second birthday. She rode Formule 1 with her partner. Near the end of the ride, over two bumps, she was lifted out of her seat. Her partner grabbed her and held on, but couldn't keep his grip. She fell, and paramedics couldn't save her.

The investigation found that the seat belts added after 2009 had been taken off a month earlier, when the park reopened after lockdown. Someone had also ground down the first notch on every lap bar so the largest guests couldn't ride. Neither change was reported to inspectors. The owner, Gilles Campion, admitted both.

In November 2025 a court in Beauvais gave him a three-year suspended sentence and banned him from running parks for five years. That same year the park was sold. In January 2026, Formule 1 was finally torn down.

**Extra material for the script**

- 2005: two defective brake pistons were seized by Beauvais prosecutors, and new brakes came from French maker Soquet (ParkOtheK, 23 Aug 2009). After the crash, SNELAC struck Campion off because he "hadn't reassured us about his capacity to ensure safety in his park" (France 3, 2020).
