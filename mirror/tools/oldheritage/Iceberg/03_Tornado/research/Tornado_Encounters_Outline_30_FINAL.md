### 01 · The Most-Watched Tornado in Science
- **Date & location:** Fri 5 Jun 2009, afternoon · Goshen County, southeastern Wyoming (near La Grange)
- **Opening visual:** A smooth funnel lowering from the base over empty Wyoming grassland.
- **What happened (research):** A supercell over Goshen County produced a tornado that became the most heavily observed in history: the VORTEX2 field project surrounded it with mobile radars and instruments, and its whole life cycle was broadcast live on The Weather Channel. It was rated EF2; official winds were over 118 mph, while mobile Doppler radar estimated 161 mph near the ground. It stayed mostly over open rural land, damaging only a few trees and telephone poles. No injuries or deaths were reported. Sources disagree on its duration (about 25 minutes in the text, 1 hour 23 minutes in the summary box).
- **Story beats:** a research armada waits · the rain shaft wraps the funnel · it touches down over open prairie · it ropes out · the most-measured tornado ever, and nobody hurt

### 02 · The Tornado Outside the Radio Studio Window
- **Date & location:** 17 Nov 2013, 3:20–3:23 pm EST · Washington, Daviess County, Indiana
- **Opening visual:** From the studio window of Memories 107.9 FM WAMW, a wall of rain and debris sweeps across the lot.
- **What happened (research):** During the 17 November 2013 outbreak, an EF2 tornado (120 mph) touched down 2.8 miles west-southwest of Washington, Indiana. It tracked 2.8 miles, up to 125 yards wide, into the city's southwest side in about three minutes, and knocked an entire house off its foundation near Fourth and Sycamore Streets. The NWS survey records no injuries and no deaths. The same outbreak produced the deadly EF4 in Washington, Illinois (case 20), a different tornado about 230 miles away.

- **What happened (research):** A strong supercell near Bowdle produced South Dakota's first EF4 since the 2003 Manchester tornado. It was on the ground about 19 minutes over 5.7 miles, up to 1,200 yards wide. At one farmstead the house lost part of its roof and walls, two large garages were destroyed and vehicles were thrown 25 to 100 yards. Six to eight steel transmission towers were toppled, one thrown an estimated 400 yards, and a state radio tower came down near SD-47. No injuries were reported.

### 05 · The Long-Track Kansas EF4, Start to Finish

- **What happened (research):** On the high-risk day of 14 April 2012, a supercell produced a long-track tornado northwest of Lyons in Rice County.
