# Most Disturbing Natural Disaster Encounters Ever Caught on Camera
Research cutoff: 31 Oct 2026 · Cases: 19 (no reserves; short of the 30 + 4 target) · Order: survivable → fatal · No tornado cases

> **Footage status:** All clips checked by 16-frame contact sheet on 1 Nov 2026 (built-in browser, own tab, muted, no audio played). **Drama pass (1 Nov 2026):** every case must show a terrifying encounter, a person, car or building in direct danger on screen. Cases rated 1–3 (wind and rain through windows or windshields, river and dam cameras, static surge streams, distant plumes) were removed; 14 cases rated 4–5 were kept and 5 new encounter cases were added. Only 19 cases reach that bar after this pass, so the list is short of 30 and has no reserves. **Small-channel rule:** every kept link comes from a channel with fewer than 50,000 subscribers, or from an official public agency (JAMSTEC #19 and Iwate Prefecture #15 are over 50k). No TV station, news outlet, publication, agency reseller, or storm-chaser/footage-licensing business is used; every kept clip's description was checked for licensing text and none has any. Overlay rule: a small corner logo, copyright line or camera date/time stamp is allowed; captions, titles, text cards, lower-thirds and narration graphics fail.

Method note: uploader, upload date, views and subscriber count come from the watch page or Algrow's channel data, read on 1 Nov 2026; duration from the video element. Facts come from the sources listed in each dossier. The fatal block rises roughly with the death toll and is shuffled so that the same disaster type doesn't run back to back (Ōfunato #15 sits before Valencia #16 for that reason). City-level tolls are used for Shiogama, Ōfunato and Kesennuma, and the national toll for Hachinohe.

### 01 · Hail Caves In the Windshield on I-70
- **Date & location:** Afternoon of Mon 8 May 2017 (the uploader puts the start at about 3:10 PM from his GPS), westbound I-70 at Wheat Ridge, west of Denver, Colorado, USA.
- **Opening visual:** From the dashboard, the first stones bounce off the hood; seconds later the glass in front of the camera starts to star and crack.
- **Drama (1 Nov 2026):** 5/5. A driver trapped on the interstate as hail breaks the windshield and he retreats from the front seat.
- **What happened (research):** A supercell crossed the west Denver metro on the afternoon of 8 May 2017 and dropped large hail across Jefferson County and Wheat Ridge during rush hour. The uploader was driving home from work on I-70 West when the storm caught him on the open highway. His dash cam keeps running as hail hammers the car, cracks and then breaks the windshield, and he moves away from the front seat while glass falls into the cabin. When the hail stops, his own camera shows the dented car and the glass inside. Insurers later put the storm's damage at about $2.3 billion, the costliest single catastrophe in Colorado history at the time. The uploader says the date and time on the camera are wrong and that he cut some of the audio; no injuries to him are reported.
- **Story beats:** rush-hour drive under a black sky → first hail on the hood → stones pound the glass and the windshield cracks → windshield gives way and the filmer gets away from it → hail eases; dented car and glass in the cabin → $2.3 billion storm
